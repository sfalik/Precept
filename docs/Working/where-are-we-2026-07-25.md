**What getting there looks like.** Your goal is for the catalogs to become canonical, and the distance is nameable. Three things earn it. First, close the declaration gaps a canonical doc already names: catalog the write surface and the nine-phase operation order, land `CertificateSteps` and `ProofVerdict`, and give `Presence`, `CountContainment` and `AssignmentQualifier` declarative sites so their minting stops being hand-written in the pipeline. Those are shape gaps in the declaration layer, not behaviour behind it, and no amount of implementation work closes them. Third, and this is the one that would actually lift the standing directive at the top of this document: build checks with an external denominator. Exactly two exist today — `CurrencyCatalogSyncTests.cs:33-45` measures the currency catalog against embedded ISO 4217 XML with a documented exclusion set, and the UCUM drift test does the same. That they are the only two is direct evidence that no other catalog is measured against anything outside itself. A catalog becomes canonical when something can fail the build for a *missing* member, not just for an unannotated one. Extending the ISO 4217 pattern to the decided surface — the spec's own enumerations as the denominator, checked in CI — is what "getting there" concretely means.
