# A Second Opinion: What Precept Should Be and What It Should Guarantee — FINAL

*Derive-first thesis, authored 2026-07-11; revised after three adversarial critiques (C1 uniqueness, C2 false-security, C3 philosophy-necessity/reconciliation) and after reading the previously-quarantined ruling cluster first-hand. Revised again same day (rev 2) per owner ruling: Precept ships only when the compiler and the runtime are both complete — there is no interim, pre-runtime window to design for, so every guarantee below is stated in end-state terms, as the final design of a completed product. The runtime's current build state is a sequencing fact, never a design input. Forward-looking throughout: no position below rests on "the code already does X." All internal citations verified first-hand at the cited lines this session or in the derivation pass. ## 1. What Precept SHOULD Be

**Precept should be the single authoritative artifact of record for what a business entity's data is allowed to become — simultaneously a specification, a proof subject, and the production enforcement engine.**

The canon names the identity: "a domain integrity engine for .NET — a single declarative contract that governs how a business entity's data evolves under business rules across its lifecycle, making invalid configurations structurally impossible" (`docs/philosophy.md:7`), with "governed integrity" as the unifying principle (`docs/philosophy.md:9`). The guarantee object is the **configuration** — "(current lifecycle position, current field values), or for stateless entities, simply the current field values" (`docs/philosophy.md:43`). Data and rules are primary; "state is instrumental, not primary" (`docs/philosophy.md:37`); stateless precepts are first-class (`docs/philosophy.md:39`).

I endorse that identity and sharpen its entailments, because the entailments — not the description — settle every downstream boundary question:

**1a. Precept should be the fused artifact, not a pipeline of separate ones.** The comparator research concedes the model is not novel: "The mapping is exact. A Precept definition is an Event-B abstract machine in .NET DSL form" (`research/philosophy/formal-spec-languages-comparators.md:164`). Event-B proves the machine and stops; Eiffel enforces contracts at runtime and proves nothing. What Precept *should* be is the third thing neither is: one file that is at once the proved definition, the deployed enforcer, and the live inspection API. §4 states precisely what is and is not proved in that fusion — the trade, not just the win.

**1b. Precept should be authored by domain experts, and that is a design constraint, not a marketing line.** "The primary author of a `.precept` file is a domain expert or a business analyst" (`docs/philosophy.md:92`). Taken seriously: every compile-time claim must be statable in one teachable sentence, every rejection must name the missing fact in domain vocabulary, and any checking posture whose boundary a domain expert cannot predict is disqualified *regardless of catch economics*. This is why the spec is right to reject opaque solvers "even when they could prove more" (`precept-language-spec.md:225`) — and it is the deciding vote on the prove-vs-govern boundary in §2.

**1c. Precept should be a governance architecture, not a better validator.** "Invoked, not structural… If no code path calls it, the rules do not apply" versus "Structural, not invoked… No code path bypasses enforcement" (`research/language/references/governance-vs-validation.md:23,32-35`), closing Bypass, Timing Gap, Scattered Rules, Silent Mutation (`governance-vs-validation.md:131-183`). Precept's value never rests on checking *more things* than FluentValidation; it rests on there being **no unchecked path**. The runtime governance leg is therefore identity-constitutive: a Precept without it is a different product, and every guarantee in this thesis is stated of the completed artifact in which that leg is fully built and enforcing (§2.4, §6).

**1d. Precept should keep the deliberately narrow execution model as a strategic asset — for three commitments, not one.** No loops, closed vocabulary, finite state space, purity, one file (`precept-language-spec.md:158-174`). These properties enable tractable solver-free proof (`precept-language-spec.md:174`) — but they equally serve **determinism** (`docs/philosophy.md:22`) and **full inspectability** (`docs/philosophy.md:60`), which are independent core commitments a runtime-only Precept would still need and still pay for. The austerity is a three-way investment; §2.7(c) accounts for it honestly rather than billing it all to proof.

**1e. Scope: one entity.** Referential integrity across entities is out of scope by design (`research/philosophy/domain-integrity-formal-concept.md:237`). Right boundary; defend it.

## 2. What Guarantees It SHOULD Provide — the Prove-vs-Govern Boundary Derived

### 2.1 The two derivation axes

**Axis 1 — Does a failed check have a graceful runtime home?** The evaluator's outcome taxonomy splits failures into a **fault** — "a mid-evaluation abort with no recoverable typed outcome (only the Faulted trap); anything the working-copy discard or ingress refusal handles (ConstraintsFailed/InvalidArgs/Unmatched/Rejected) is governance" (`research/architecture/compiler/fault-floor-definition-2026-06-11.md:122`; independently derived at `research/architecture/compiler/compile-time-guarantee-boundary-2026-06-10.md:39-42`). A failure with no graceful home cannot be governed; a failure with a typed refusal carrying its mandatory `because` is exactly what governance is for.

**Axis 2 — Is the fact decidable from the definition alone, and could any runtime input repair it?** (`compile-time-guarantee-boundary:39`). Whole-definition facts no single execution can compute; concrete-value facts no compiler can know.

Crossing the axes yields four dispositions:

| Disposition | What lands here | Posture |
|---|---|---|
| **PROVE (reject if unprovable)** | Evaluation-fault freedom — the fault floor | Prove-or-reject, total, no deferral |
| **PROVE (reject on proven violation)** | Definition incoherence — self-contradicting definitions | Always-Error; a third category |
| **FLAG (warn on proven violation only)** | Universal definition-quality facts beyond the floor | Flag-sound warnings; silence promises nothing |
| **GOVERN (enforce at runtime)** | Concrete-value satisfaction of every declared constraint | Ingress + commit-time atomic enforcement |

### 2.2 PROVE, no deferral: the fault floor

Faults — the eleven families enumerated at `fault-floor-definition:124` (division/modulo divisor, √/pow non-negativity, unset-optional read, empty-collection access ×2, index bounds, key presence, qualifier compatibility, decimal representability, temporal representability, computed-arg computability) — must be proven absent at compile time or the definition rejected. Three identity-derived reasons:

1. **Axis 1 forces it.** A fault fires mid-plan before any constraint verdict exists; the only runtime "handling" is an abort. Principle 10: "Runtime fault traps exist only as defensive redundancy for paths the compiler has already proven unreachable" (`precept-language-spec.md:110`). A prevention product cannot ship a failure mode whose only appearance is an unrecoverable abort.

2. **Inspection honesty forces it.** The authoring loop evaluates full machinery per keystroke; for a live "Certain" verdict to be honest, fault-freedom must already be proven, and "Possible" must denote only awaiting-external-input, never "the engine declined to analyze" (`compile-time-guarantee-boundary:66-72`). This gives the floor its stopping rule: *the proof engine must reach exactly far enough that the only thing left unknown to live inspection is genuinely-external input not yet supplied* (`compile-time-guarantee-boundary:72`).

3. **The composition seam makes prove-or-reject compatible with external input.** "The compiler proves a structural fact — that the value carries its constraint — never the value itself; the runtime enforcement is what makes that carried constraint true, not a second line of defense" (`docs/philosophy.md:55`; operationalized at `precept-language-spec.md:270`). This is the mainstream verifier posture — Liquid Haskell's refined divisor (`research/architecture/compiler/interval-vs-value-evaluation-prior-art-2026-06-05.md:83`), SPARK's supplied preconditions (`:118-121`) — with Precept's twist that the caller obligation is discharged *automatically by its own ingress governance* rather than by a human at every call site.

**Three consequences the floor's own logic demands:**

(i) **Position-totality is part of the claim.** An obligation must exist at *every* fault-prone expression position or Principle 11's sentence ("If a precept compiles without diagnostics, it does not fault at runtime," `precept-language-spec.md:112`) is false; ten live fail-open positions are documented (`fault-floor-definition:126,140`) and must be closed, not annotated.

(ii) **Constraint discharge must be write-aware and actually enforced.** A standing constraint may discharge a fault obligation only if no unaccounted same-transaction write can falsify it, and only if the discharging constraint is genuinely enforced at every ingress/commit lane (`fault-floor-definition:128`). A proof whose precondition governance does not actually make true is not a proof — it is the seam of `philosophy.md:55` quietly severed. The documented zero-consumer state of that enforcement wiring (`fault-floor-definition:144`) is a build gap the floor's soundness requires closed — on the same footing as the fail-open positions in (i), and like them a completeness obligation, not a design fork.

(iii) **The overflow families are the identity-correct target — and they are blocked, and the interim claim is currently overclaimed.** *(Revised per C3-F4/F5.)* Representability overflow (decimal and temporal) belongs on the floor by Axis 1 — it aborts, gracelessly — and by Principle 10's own enumeration (`:110`). But it is "THE one missing obligation family … gated on the owner's numeric-representation decision" (`fault-floor-definition:124` item 9): fixed-decimal-plus-obligation-bounds versus arbitrary-precision numerics, where arbitrary precision *deletes the fault class* (`compiler-readiness-plan-2026-06-16.md:195`). The D1 park (`compiler-readiness-plan:188-201`) is a legitimate **scheduling** act — I do not contest sequencing an unbuilt family behind an unmade prerequisite decision. What identity does not permit is the *interim claim*: `philosophy.md:53` states verbatim that "Division by zero, **arithmetic overflow**, empty collection access … are compile-time impossibilities," and Principles 10/11 (`:110`,`:112`) list overflow among prevented fault classes — none of which is true for representational overflow today, and D1's own text says "never claim it prevented" (`compiler-readiness-plan:191`). Owner-gated recommendation D (below) reconciles those sentences for the interim; the build-to-target posture keeps overflow *in* the floor's definition as the completion target, contingent on the number-model decision the owner has not yet made — a decision this thesis surfaces rather than assumes.

### 2.3 PROVE on proven violation: definition incoherence

A definition whose declared defaults violate its own declared rules, or whose initial state is unsatisfiable, is broken in a way no runtime input can repair — Axis 2's pure case (`compile-time-guarantee-boundary:48`). Always-Error, and a **third category** — "proven self-contradictions where no valid Create exists" (`fault-floor-definition:132`) — distinct from faults and flags. This category requires value-level reasoning, not just intervals: the concrete default-fold catches seven of eight broken-default shapes bounds-only reasoning passes clean (`research/architecture/compiler/bounds-only-constraint-enforcement-2026-06-05.md:40-47,64-79,147-152`); the two mechanisms are complementary (`bounds-only:142`).

**The membership criterion, stated explicitly** *(added per C3-F3)*: incoherence is the category for contradictions that make **the entity itself impossible or the definition self-falsifying** — no valid Create exists (default-vs-bound, unsatisfiable initial state), or the definition asserts two things that cannot both hold. A provably-dead *operation row* on an otherwise-valid entity is **not** in this category: a valid entity exists, every configuration it can reach is valid, and the defect is a dead limb — an authored operation that can never succeed — which is the flag layer's genus (§2.5). Rationale: the categories track *what is impossible* (the entity vs. one operation's success). Alternative rejected: uniform Error on both (Frank's Q6/Q5 posture, `proof-engine-boundary-ruling:174-183`) — coherent, but it rejects a usable definition for containing a dead limb, the same over-reach as rejecting a definition for a dead guard, and it erases the line between "no valid entity can exist" and "one path is dead." Precedent: the dead-guard family is specified as detection-and-report, not rejection (`precept-language-spec.md:211`, §0.6 item 9). Tradeoff accepted: a definition containing an operation the compiler has proven can never succeed ships with a warning — the owner may judge that too permissive, and **this severity fork is a genuine two-sided owner decision** I mark as such (§6, Recommendation H): `philosophy.md:43` ("Invalid configurations are structurally impossible") can be read either way, and the readiness plan's own cross-transition-ensure reasoning ("a complete runtime cannot rescue a never-satisfiable ensure," `compiler-readiness-plan:1097`) argues the other side. I state my position and its reason; I do not pretend the fork is closed.

### 2.4 GOVERN: concrete-value satisfaction — including declared bands

Every declared constraint is enforced at runtime on every value entering through the contract, atomically, on a working copy discarded on failure (`precept-language-spec.md:268,149`). This is prevention — the invalid configuration never exists — and it is the half of the guarantee that closes the four validator failure modes. For a product whose category claim is governance-not-validation (`precept-language-spec.md:140-142`), the runtime leg *is* the headline.

**The contested cell: containment of a *derived* value within a declared band.** The spec currently asserts prove-or-reject in three places, quoted verbatim in Recommendation A. My derivation — reached under quarantine, before reading the ruling — is that non-proof-carrying band containment belongs in GOVERN, and I now restate it split into its two genuinely distinct sub-questions *(per C3-F3)*, because they belong to different categories:

**(i) The proven-always-violating write** (e.g. literal `set F = 200` into `max 100`). This is a dead operation row: every fire is refused by the runtime; no invalid configuration is ever produced — but the row is provably inert. By §2.3's criterion it is flag-layer (a true universal statement about a dead limb), not incoherence and not a fault. The default-vs-set-action asymmetry the D2 analysis surfaced (`docs/Working/d2-proven-violation-philosophy-analysis-2026-07-03.md:77`) is resolved by the criterion, not left as an anomaly: a violating **default** means *no valid Create exists* → incoherence, Error; a violating **set-action** leaves a valid entity with one dead operation → flag, Warning. The "when" (creation vs. transition) is not the distinguisher — *what becomes impossible* is.

**(ii) The merely-unprovable write** (unbounded interval into a bounded field) — the real prevention-vs-false-security crux. Derivation: constraint modifiers are rule shorthand — "`min 5` and `rule X >= 5` are interchangeable to the proof engine" (`precept-language-spec.md:1138`); a band violation at runtime resolves to `ConstraintsFailed`, a recoverable governed refusal, which by the evaluator's own consequence taxonomy (`fault-floor-definition:122`) is governance's home turf, not a fault. The runtime enforces containment on every write, on every path; prevention is relocated, not weakened (`compiler-readiness-plan:213`) — the invalid configuration still never exists, checked at the moment a concrete value arrives rather than approximated over all possible values in advance. Prove-or-reject here over-rejects and forces bound-invention — "representability masquerading as policy," anti-honest by Principle 8 (`docs/Working/compile-time-niche-decision-packet-2026-06-10.md:129`; corpus: 214 unbounded money fields vs 10 bounded, `bounds-only:120-122`). **With one principled exception:** a bound that *discharges a fault obligation* is proof-carrying — writes into it must prove preservation or reject (`fault-floor-definition:132,177`). The boundary is proof-carrying vs. not, never band vs. not. Note the owner's count-bound precedent (`c27a382b`, Reading A) sits *on the proof-carrying side of this line* — `mincount` discharges the count>0 access-safety obligation — so the precedent supports the exception more directly than it supports blanket rejection.

**The legibility of the classification, worked through the primary author's eyes** *(added per C2-F3)*: a domain expert writes `min 5` and will naturally read it as "the system enforces this" — bounds are the constraint shape most likely to be mentally filed as compiler-checked. What she must see, *at the constraint, ambiently, not behind a query*: on a non-proof-carrying bound — "GOVERNED: every write is checked against `min 5` at the moment it enters; a violating write is refused with this rule's stated reason. Not proven in advance." On a proof-carrying bound — "PROVEN: any write the compiler cannot prove within bounds is rejected at compile time." Both sentences must be true of the shipped product by construction (§2.7(b)); the classification and its legibility are one deliverable, not two.

### 2.5 FLAG on proven violation: the universal-quality layer

Beyond the floor lies a defect family nothing else can catch: dead reject rows whose failure signature is a clean wrong success; contradictory/vacuous rules; guard-locked strands defeating "no workflow trapped in a state it can never leave" (`docs/philosophy.md:49`); clock-decay traps; decision-band gaps (`niche-packet:23-69`). The spec commits to most of this (§0.6 items 7–12, `precept-language-spec.md:209-214,239-247`); identity says these must ship — an authored prohibition silently inert is a governance-integrity breach.

Equally: they ship as **flags under a flag-soundness contract** — "a flag is always a true universal statement about the definition; silence beyond the fault floor promises nothing" (`niche-packet:117`). Three reasons: (1) the fragment boundary is invisible to the primary author — the same relation written three ways gets three guarantee levels; parameterizing a constant silently exits the fragment (`niche-packet:184`) — and a rejection posture over an invisible boundary fails §1b; (2) prove-or-reject beyond the floor makes compiler upgrades breaking events (`niche-packet:117,184`); (3) proven-violations-only is the stated proof philosophy — "when it speaks, it is right" (`precept-language-spec.md:223`).

Non-negotiable precondition: the flags must actually be flag-sound. A live probe showed PRE0155 firing a *false* universal claim on a satisfiable idiomatic policy (`niche-packet:117,171`). One false universal does more damage than a hundred missing ones.

### 2.6 Separate lane, named honestly

Definition-evolution safety (a tightened rule stranding a persisted fleet, both versions compiling clean) is the most severe uncovered family and is structurally a two-definition deploy-gate problem (`niche-packet:106-108`). Schedule it as its own decision before first external users.

### 2.7 The three owner tensions, explicitly resolved

**(a) Should MORE be pushed into compile-time proof — and is that practical?**

Split by what "more" means. *More coverage of the existing claim*: unambiguously yes — position-totality, write-aware discharge, the false-'Proved' repairs, and (once the number model is decided) the representability family are the difference between Principle 11 being true and false. Completing a promise, not extending one. *More rejection posture beyond the floor*: no. The strongest maximalist evidence (inspector-blind silent-wrong-success defects in hand-polished samples; DMN-style gap/overlap value, `niche-packet:139-156`) argues for the **flag layer existing**, not for rejection. The ceiling is threefold: (i) the most business-severe class — wrong-but-in-band formulas and constants — "is undecidable for ANY compile-time system" (`niche-packet:90-92`); (ii) the tractable fragment is provably blind to equality, disjunction, three-variable sums (`bounds-only:40-47`; Miné per `interval-vs-value-evaluation-prior-art:62-63`), and SMT is rightly forbidden (`precept-language-spec.md:225`); (iii) rejection ceremony (bound-invention) taxes every author on day one (`niche-packet:129,167`). **Resolution: maximal proof coverage of the fault floor and incoherence; maximal flag-sound universal analysis above it; zero rejection-posture expansion beyond proof-carrying constraints.**

**(b) Does partial compile-time guarantee create false security — and what is the right response?**

*(Restructured per C2-F1/F2/F4/F5: disclosures first, then the design, marked as hypothesis.)*

Start with the hard facts, because the honesty program must apply to itself. **First, a lying ledger is the only genuine false-security machine here, and the current implementation has one**: three verified false-'Proved' sites (`niche-packet:117,182`) plus ten fail-open obligation positions (`fault-floor-definition:126,140`) plus write-unaware tier-2 discharge (`fault-floor-definition:128`). These outrank all breadth work — and they bound the trustworthiness of *any* legibility mechanism, because a per-rule "PROVEN" tag is only as sound as the engine that computes it. **A sound ledger is therefore a required property of the completed product, not a sequencing step**: every PROVEN tag corresponds to a real, coverage-complete proof, and every GOVERNED tag corresponds to real, structural enforcement — a disposition surface never asserts what the engine does not deliver. **Second**, the philosophy itself carries the exact "absolutist copy beside a precise claim" configuration the literature warns about: `docs/philosophy.md:49` opens *"Prevention, not detection. No errors. No bugs. Business logic cannot produce a wrong answer"* while `:57` states the precise, correct scope. "No bugs" is falsified by the product's own concession that wrong-but-in-band values are undecidable — Recommendation B tempers it.

Against that backdrop, the literature's finding: partial static coverage misleads in one configuration — **when the boundary is illegible** — and mitigations that measurably work are structural (SPARK's named assurance levels; Dafny's greppable `assert`/`assume`; a check materializing exactly where static proof stops — Bader/Aldrich/Tanter, VMCAI 2018), while prose caveats are cosmetic (Lee & See 2004; Tunnell Wilson et al., DLS 2018 — even *sound* diagnostics were misjudged by engineers, a fortiori by Precept's less-technical audience). Precept's spec is unusually well-positioned as raw material: Principle 4 extends inspectability to "what the engine could not prove" (`precept-language-spec.md:98`); §0.6 #6 mandates structured proof attribution (`:231`); #5's tri-state matches per-obligation disposition (`:229`). But — conceding C2's sharpest point — **spec clauses describing a capability are not the capability working on this audience.** The dominant overtrust vector is a reader generalizing the ambient "compiles clean" signal across every constraint without ever querying a secondary panel. So the design bar, stated as a bar and not a solved problem: the disposition ("proven / governed / flagged") must be **ambient at the point of authorship** — rendered inline at each constraint, not behind a hover or query — and the residual risk that no marker can close must be named: an undecidable wrong-but-in-band constant produces the same zero-diagnostic surface as no defect at all; the live inspector is its only catch, and nothing at the point of writing the literal marks it as outside every analytic fragment. Whether a per-expression "inspector-only" marker is worth its noise is a real open design question. Both the surface design and that marker are **untested hypotheses routed to `/design`** (Recommendation G), with the false-security question named as the acceptance criterion, not assumed answered.

**Resolution — four required properties of the completed product: a sound ledger; every GOVERNED tag backed by real enforcement; an ambient, legible per-obligation boundary; precisely-scoped claims.** Not "more proof" (the ceiling in (a) means completeness cannot close the gap), and not narrower claims alone.

**(c) Would relaxing everything to runtime governance leave Precept without a unique enough value proposition?**

Not *nothing* — the governance architecture survives as a differentiated product: structural, lifecycle-aware, one-file, inspectable enforcement is genuinely absent from the landscape (`research/product/entity-governance-landscape.md:416-430`), and the four failure modes it closes are real (`governance-vs-validation.md:131-183`).

What dies, honestly accounted *(revised per C1-F5)*: **two constitutive losses and one weaker economic one.** First, the **process half of the headline guarantee is compile-time by construction** — reachability, dead-end freedom, required-state dominance are whole-definition facts no runtime instance can compute (`docs/philosophy.md:51`; `compile-time-guarantee-boundary:47`); relaxation deletes "no workflow trapped in a state it can never leave," not weakens it. Second, **inspection honesty collapses**: without the fault floor, a per-keystroke "Certain" verdict can crash or lie, degrading the product's central authoring surface from oracle to preview. Third — and this is the corrected, weaker claim — the §0.4 language austerity would *not* be refunded: determinism and inspectability retain most of it. The economic argument is not "sunk cost returned" but "the incremental cost of the proof leg, given a language already this shape, is small relative to what it buys."

The precedent line, hedged where the evidence is: the nearest analog to runtime-only entity-bound contracts is design-by-contract — Eiffel and .NET Code Contracts — and both failed commercially in that posture. *Caveat (per C1-F6): the specific Eiffel-monitoring-dial evidence is external synthesis (eiffel.org, via R1/R3) I could not re-verify against an in-repo artifact, and the precedent cuts both ways — if DbC failed on ergonomics and missing static proof, it is evidence for Precept's fused design, not against runtime enforcement per se.* What the precedent does establish: a runtime-only Precept enters a category with a documented commercial corpse and without the property that distinguishes it from a revived Code Contracts with a lifecycle model.

**Resolution: the fault floor + incoherence rejection + flag-sound universal layer is the minimum proof leg the identity requires.** Whether the proof leg becomes the *marketed identity* or remains an unmarketed quality layer is a live owner choice the niche packet explicitly leaves open (`niche-packet:133` caveat 6) — this thesis establishes proof as constitutive to the *product*, and stays neutral on the *marketing* register.

## 3. Where It Fits — the Category Map and the Owned Seam

| Lane | Representatives | What they own | What is structurally absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure validators | FluentValidation, Zod, JSON Schema | Rule vocabulary, ecosystem | Structural enforcement (invoked, bypassable — `governance-vs-validation.md:43,64`); no lifecycle |
| State machines | Stateless, XState, Spring SM | Transition legality | No field model or data enforcement (`docs/philosophy.md:69`) |
| Rules engines / BRMS | Drools, NRules, DMN | Decision logic; business-user authorship; hot redeploy | No entity binding, mutation, lifecycle (`docs/philosophy.md:70`) |
| Policy engines | OPA, Cedar | Decoupled allow/deny | Caller must honor; no entity/lifecycle (`docs/philosophy.md:73`) |
| Design-by-contract | Eiffel, .NET Code Contracts | Entity-bound structural invariants | Runtime-monitor-only; no lifecycle; commercially discontinued in .NET |
| Formal methods | Event-B, TLA+, Alloy, Dafny | Design-time proof | No runtime artifact; specialist audience (`formal-spec-languages-comparators.md:139-142`) |
| Enterprise platforms | Salesforce, Guidewire | The full combination — as a platform | Not embeddable (`docs/philosophy.md:74`) |
| DB constraints | CHECK/triggers | Storage-layer structural enforcement — **runtime**, not compile-time | No lifecycle, inspectability, app-layer tooling (`governance-vs-validation.md:107-111`) |

**The seam, recast as cost-to-replicate rather than occupancy** *(revised per C1-F3)*: the six-property conjunction (*entity-bound + lifecycle-aware + structurally non-bypassable + embeddable + definition-proved + inspectable*) is unoccupied (`entity-governance-landscape.md:430`) — but an empty cell is near-guaranteed by any long-enough conjunction, and each property is individually occupied by someone. The defensible question is not "who occupies the cell" but "what does it cost a fast follower to fuse the properties." A validator can bolt on a state chart in a quarter. What it cannot bolt on cheaply: a language austere enough for solver-free proof (a ground-up language decision, §0.4), a proof ledger legible to domain experts (a multi-year attribution architecture, §0.6 #5/#6), and one-file semantics where the proved artifact *is* the deployed enforcer. The moat, to the extent one exists, lives in that fusion cost — not in the cell count. Where the fusion cost is low (XState + rollback covers "lifecycle + non-bypass" — XState "stops short of" it today, `entity-governance-landscape.md:360`), the positioning must expect fast followers.

Four caveats carried, not buried: (i) the landscape memo is company-commissioned positioning research; (ii) the DbC lane shows structural entity-bound enforcement was tried in .NET and discontinued — and the philosophy's comparator table omits DbC entirely (`docs/philosophy.md:65-77`; Eiffel appears nowhere) — Recommendation C adds the row; (iii) "domain integrity engine" is a category-creation play with an unengaged "why not a bolt-on?" objection (`entity-governance-landscape.md:482`); (iv) BRMS buyers value redeploy-free rule change, which a compiled source-controlled `.precept` file does not answer — live product question.

Complements, not competitors: orchestrators route, Precept constrains; event sourcing stores, Precept validates first; the ORM persists, Precept governs before the write (`docs/philosophy.md:80-84`). Keep that framing aggressively.

## 4. What Makes It Unique — Engaging "Event-B Is Structurally Identical," With the Trade Stated

The comparator finding is true and embraced: "The mapping is exact… Same model, different derivation path, different deployment target" (`formal-spec-languages-comparators.md:164`). Abrial's state-variables/invariants/guarded-events is the correct minimal model; claiming novelty of the model would be false. The differentiator is threefold — and I now state it with the trade C1 correctly demanded:

**4a. The fused deployment target — with the proof-scope trade made explicit.** *(Revised per C1-F1/F2.)* On the invariant both systems share — "rules hold in every reachable state" — **Event-B out-proves Precept by design**: it discharges a deductive proof obligation per event that invariants are preserved (`formal-spec-languages-comparators.md:168`), where "Event-B's guarantee is deductive (formal proof); Precept's is operational (runtime enforcement)" (`:168`). Precept's *proof* surface is deliberately smaller: fault-freedom and definition-coherence (§2.2–2.3), with business invariants **enforced**, not proved (§2.4). The honest trade: Precept gives up Event-B's invariant-preservation proof and receives, in exchange, (a) runtime enforcement with no refinement gap — in Event-B the proof exists and a human re-implements the machine; in Precept the artifact that was checked *is* the deployed machine, so nothing proved can diverge from anything running; (b) a solver-free fault proof a non-specialist can read; (c) a live production inspection API. Nor is "static floor + deployed runtime" alone the invention — SPARK ships exactly that, proving absence-of-runtime-errors in production binaries today (`interval-vs-value-evaluation-prior-art:114-126`). **The narrow, defensible claim leads:** in SPARK, Dafny, and Liquid Haskell, the fault proof's precondition is discharged by a human supplying it at every call site (or by an unsound escape hatch); in Eiffel the runtime check is a switchable second line of defense; **in Precept, uniquely among the surveyed systems, the proof's precondition is discharged automatically by the artifact's own ingress governance — the runtime check is not a fallback but the discharge mechanism of the proof itself** ("proven by the compiler, its precondition discharged by governance, nothing left to a runtime check," `precept-language-spec.md:270`), and it is constitutionally non-optional. That composition seam — not the abstract machine, and not the bare static-floor-plus-runtime combination — is the invention.

**4b. Legible proof for a non-specialist author.** Every proving comparator targets engineers or formal-methods specialists; Event-B "requires formal methods training" (`formal-spec-languages-comparators.md:91`). Precept's principled solver rejection (`precept-language-spec.md:225`), structured attribution (`:231`), tri-state classification (`:229`), and mandatory authored rationale (`:108`) constitute a proof system designed to be read by the rule's owner. No external comparator exists for that posture (`niche-packet:133` caveat 5) — simultaneously the opportunity and the unmeasured risk.

**4c. Inspection as a runtime capability of the deployed artifact.** `Inspect` — non-mutating, complete, per-event outcome preview in production (`docs/philosophy.md:18,60`) — has no analog: ProB is a development animator, not a production API (`formal-spec-languages-comparators.md:178`); validators and DB constraints are probed only by attempting the operation (`governance-vs-validation.md:108`).

One sentence, technical register: *Precept is the Event-B abstract machine shipped as its own production runtime — trading deductive invariant-preservation proof for gap-free operational enforcement, with the fault proof's preconditions discharged by the runtime's own governance and the whole reasoning chain, static and live, exposed as structured data legible to the rule's owner.*

## 5. The Value Proposition

Three concentric claims, each mapped to a mechanism:

**The replacement claim (entry point):** replace Stateless + FluentValidation + the glue between them with one `.precept` file where lifecycle and data rules are co-defined (`entity-governance-landscape.md:450-463`). The pain is documented and convergent across ecosystems; the 10:1 FluentValidation:Stateless download ratio locates the funnel (`entity-governance-landscape.md:434-446`).

**The governance claim (the category):** not "we check thoroughly" but "the invalid configuration cannot exist" (`governance-vs-validation.md:195`) — every rule, every operation, atomically, with a mandatory stated reason, no bypass path. One artifact is enforcement, documentation, and audit trail — a rules register in which a dead rule is a compiler-reported defect rather than dead text.

**The proof claim (the bet we are making, stated as one):** *(revised per C1-F4)* the definition itself is checked sound before any entity exists. This is a **prospective differentiator, not a demonstrated moat**: by the product's own research the current proof leg's catch-rate against idiomatic corpora "is honestly near zero," the case resting on "floors-plus-demand, not measured corpus incidence" (`niche-packet:121,133` caveats 1,3). What makes it worth betting on is §4's fusion-cost argument and the external demand evidence — but a thesis that sells calibrated trust must calibrate its own label.

**The teachable claim — three sentences, three registers, never one breath.** *(Revised per C2-F2: the previous single-sentence bundling of PROVE/FLAG/GOVERN is precisely the configuration the false-security literature says readers collapse into the strongest clause. The tiers must stay visually and rhetorically distinct, SPARK-style:)*

1. **Proven** — *"If your precept compiles, no operation can ever fault."* (Universal, static — true only after the floor's coverage gaps and false-'Proved' sites are closed, `niche-packet:133` caveat 4.)
2. **Flagged** — *"Anything the compiler flags is proven wrong for every possible entity. Silence beyond that promises nothing."* (Universal but partial, static — the second sentence is part of the claim, not a footnote.)
3. **Governed** — *"Everything your actual data decides is checked on every operation, at the moment it happens, with a stated reason."* (Per-instance, dynamic — the claim IS the runtime's structural enforcement; a GOVERNED sentence not backed by real enforcement is a false-security defect, §2.7(b).)

Any marketing collapse of the three into one flowing guarantee is itself a false-security defect and should be treated as such in copy review.

The meta-proposition binding them: **Precept sells calibrated trust.** A product whose per-obligation disposition — proven, governed, or flagged — is an ambient, structured, honest surface offers what no adjacent lane can: not just enforcement, but a guarantee you can interrogate. That is the product the philosophy's principles, taken all the way forward, actually describe — provided the disposition ledger is sound (§2.7(b)).
