## Showing its work (a readable, checkable proof for every result)

**1. What it proves.** Nothing new by itself — this is the shared machinery underneath every other proof on this list. It makes the compiler attach a short, plain-language justification to every "proved" and every "rejected" verdict, and adds a small independent double-check that re-reads each justification and confirms it actually holds. The compiler never just says "trust me" — in either direction.

**2. What gets rejected without it.** Nothing extra — this capability changes what a verdict *says*, not which definitions pass. To be honest about that up front: no `.precept` file is rejected "for lack of a justification." Where it bites is in what the author sees. Take this real excerpt from `samples/invoice-line-item.precept` with one word added (`nonnegative` on the computed field, synthetic addition labeled):

```precept
field UnitPrice as money in '{CurrencyCode}' default '0 {CurrencyCode}' nonnegative editable
field DiscountPercent as decimal default 0 nonnegative max 100 maxplaces 2 editable

field Subtotal as money in '{CurrencyCode}' <- UnitPrice * Quantity
field DiscountAmount as money in '{CurrencyCode}' <- Subtotal * DiscountPercent / 100
field TaxableAmount as money in '{CurrencyCode}' nonnegative <- Subtotal - DiscountAmount
```

Under the PROPOSED design this is rejected — the compiler's range arithmetic treats `Subtotal` and `DiscountAmount` as independent, so it cannot see that the discount never exceeds the subtotal. (Today: compiles clean — this posture isn't built; the compiler currently generates *zero* proof checks for that `nonnegative`, verified live against the current engine.) Without show-your-work, the rejection reads roughly "cannot prove TaxableAmount is nonnegative" — and the author, who *knows* a ≤100% discount can't push the amount negative, has no way to tell whether the compiler found a real hole or is just being cautious. With it, the verdict carries the chain: "DiscountPercent may reach 100, I track Subtotal and DiscountAmount as separate ranges, so their difference could — as far as I can see — go below zero." Now the author knows exactly which link to attack. The same applies to *accepted* definitions: today a successful check reports only the name of the recipe that proved it (observed live: "Proved — DeclarationAttribute — Divisor must be non-zero"), not reasoning anyone could re-check.

**3. How the author clears it today, by hand.** Grade: **CAN'T-BE-DONE-BY-HAND.** There is no `.precept` construct that supplies, demands, or displays a justification — the language has nothing to write. The author's only substitute is re-deriving the compiler's reasoning mentally from the one-line diagnostic and the docs. (The *underlying rejections* each have hand-fixes covered in their own sections above — e.g. for a can-be-zero divisor, changing `nonnegative` to `positive` on the divisor field flips the verdict to proved, verified live on today's engine. But that fixes the math, not the missing explanation.)

**4. Cost to build.** Medium. A surprising amount of the raw material already exists: the compiler already records, for every check, whether it passed, which recipe passed it, the numeric range it computed, and a link back to the exact spot in the file (`src/Precept/Pipeline/ProofLedger.cs:24–31`, `:117`) — and the language-server and MCP tools already consume those records (`ProofLedger.cs:6–31,100`). There is also an existing expression pretty-printer to render formulas back in the author's own vocabulary (`ProofEngine.cs:986`). What's genuinely new: (a) each of the 11 existing proof recipes (`docs/compiler/proof-engine.md:710–1727`) must emit its actual reasoning steps, not just its name — all of them flow through one dispatch point (`ProofEngine.cs:1054`), so there's a single seam to wire; (b) a written-down justification format; (c) the small independent re-checker itself. The main risk: the re-checker must stay strictly simpler than the prover, and every future proof recipe inherits an "emit your reasoning" obligation — a permanent tax on all later proof work. That tax is the point, but it's real.

**5. Benefit.** This rides on every proof, so its frequency is the sum of all the others: across the 77 files in `samples/`, 14 files carry 33 computed-field formulas, and there are 149 rule/ensure lines and roughly 100 declared min/max bounds — every one becomes a proof site under prove-or-reject, and every one produces a verdict this capability makes checkable. The hand-work it removes is not edits to `.precept` files but the re-derivation loop: today, every rejection the author disagrees with means reverse-engineering the compiler's reasoning from a one-line message. In a solo-dev, pre-release setting the deeper benefit is to the *builder*: an independent re-check that fails loudly whenever a proof recipe drifts is the cheapest standing defense against the engine quietly accepting something it didn't actually prove.

**6. Verdict: PREREQUISITE** — retrofitting "show your reasoning" under 11 already-built recipes plus every new one later costs strictly more than laying the format down before the new proof types land, and prove-or-reject's whole bargain with the author (a rejection you can argue with, an acceptance you can check) rests on it.
