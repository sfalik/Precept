## Part 1 — Machinery enumeration

Terms used below:

- **Obligation** — a single thing the compiler must prove (e.g., "this divisor is non-zero", "this write keeps the field inside its declared bound"). The engine's term of record is `ProofObligation` (`src/Precept/Pipeline/ProofLedger.cs:24`).
- **Premise** — a fact the proof is allowed to use: a field modifier, an event-argument constraint, a handler's guard, or a rule assumed to hold in the pre-state (want doc § The compiler's promise, four premise classes).
- **Certificate** — the recorded derivation of a proof: theorem, premises used, and steps, each step re-checkable in bounded time (want doc § Certificates).
- **Single-hop restriction** — the currently locked spec rule that relational reasoning applies one declared relation at a time and never chains through a third field (`docs/language/precept-language-spec.md:256`, § 0.6 "single-pass and depth-bounded (no transitive chasing of a third field)").

### 1.2 Does any of this require new proof theory?

**No.** Every obligation class the want doc generates falls inside fragments that have been decidable and industrially practiced for decades:

- **The logic fragment.** The language is not Turing-complete: no loops, no functions, no recursion (want doc § What must not compile, closing paragraph; spec § 0.8 implication 5 "No iteration constructs"; spec § 0.4 execution-model properties). Every handler is a finite, loop-free chain of assignments; every rule/guard is a quantifier-free formula over a handful of fields. Rule preservation for such a handler is a single Hoare-triple check — substitute the assignment into the post-state rule and prove one quantifier-free implication. For linear conditions over rationals/decimals this is decidable by variable elimination (Fourier–Motzkin, 1826/1936) with checkable Farkas-multiplier certificates (Farkas' lemma, 1902); for integer subjects, the bounded few-variable instances here are trivially within reach. No fixpoints are ever needed, because there is nothing to iterate: the induction is over *events*, and the finite state machine × finite handler rows gives a **finite** obligation set.
- **The inductive shape.** "Base case at construction + every event preserves every invariant" is exactly the proof-obligation scheme of **Event-B/Rodin** (invariant-preservation obligations generated per event, discharged mostly automatically) and of inductive invariant checking in TLA+/Apalache. The compiler-integrated variant is what **SPARK/GNATprove** and refinement-type systems (Liquid Haskell) do daily on far richer languages, using the same decidable linear-arithmetic core. Precedent already surveyed in-repo: `docs/Working/proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` § 2 ("Prior art" — SPARK, Liquid Haskell, octagons/polyhedra, Astrée).
- **Certificates and the small checker.** Proof-carrying code (Necula & Lee — cited by the want doc itself, § Certificates) and the SAT world's DRAT/LRAT formats (primary-source mirrors in `research/references/witness-checking/`, cited in `certificate-steps-membership-2026-07-12.md` § Language Design Grounding) are the established pattern: an unconstrained prover, a tiny checker that replays bounded steps. The 21-step vocabulary with per-step recompute rules is already designed to that pattern.
- **The one genuine boundary: nonlinearity.** The surface admits multiplication and division of variables (`-Balance / PlanRepayment.Months`). General nonlinear arithmetic is not decidable over integers and expensive over reals. Precept's existing answer — sign-set reasoning (`ProofEngine.Composition.cs`, sign resolution) plus prove-or-reject — is the standard practical treatment, and it already discharges the want doc's own nonlinear case (baseline: "Divisor must be non-zero" Proved via `CompositionalConstraint`, § 2.2). The consequence to state honestly: **some true-but-hard files will be rejected and asked for premises.** That is not a soundness hole — it is exactly the trade the want doc authorizes ("the guarantee is non-negotiable; the surface is the negotiable part", § Expressibility trades for proof). The engineering discipline it demands is completeness *management* (which shapes the bounded strategies must cover so author friction stays tolerable), which is product tuning, not proof theory.

### 1.3 Sizing and the one-person-plus-agents assessment

**Routine engineering** (well-understood, mostly enumeration and wiring):

- Obligation generation for rule establishment (base case) and rule preservation (inductive step), including the symmetric write-site attachment (#2, #3, #6) — the substrate (write-site enumeration, rule-reference tracking, the discharge cascade) all exists.
- Counter-premise computation for linear obligations (#10) — backward substitution; the diagnostic scaffolding already does it for counts.
- Severity flips and guard-vs-rule satisfiability (#11, #12).
- Ingress-surface derivation from certificates (#13).
- Certificate format + verdict DU (#8) — large (the readiness plan sizes Slice 0 "L" with a breaking ledger reshape migrating ~3,600 test fixtures) but fully designed with per-step recompute rules and a coverage table over every existing discharge path.

**Hard but bounded** (real design/soundness risk, still no open theory):

- The multi-term linear fact core ("§1a"): ~500–700 engine LOC + 1,000–1,600 test LOC, inside the locked single-hop spec (`proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` § 1; independently re-derived in `prior-engineering-assessment.md` § 2 — both docs agree, and their code anchors were spot-verified this session).
- Fact combination ("§1b", Fourier–Motzkin + Farkas certificates + independent checker + counterexamples + re-derived staleness discipline): ~1,500–2,200 code LOC + 2,500–4,000 test LOC, gated on **two owner spec decisions** — overriding the locked single-hop sentence (`precept-language-spec.md:256`) and ratifying the Farkas certificate as legible (`:225`) (`proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` §§ 2, 5). **Consequence of the want doc**: general rule preservation with "all pre-state rules" as hypotheses is precisely the shape the prior assessment flagged as making §1b's need "immediate" rather than prospective ("If rule-preservation-after-write ever becomes a generated obligation … §1b's need stops being prospective and becomes immediate", `prior-engineering-assessment.md` § 1 caveat; same conclusion `proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` § 2). The want doc's own worked example needs only one-fact-plus-intervals reasoning per handler (each proof consumes the rule *or* the guard *or* an arg bound, one at a time — § Worked example), so §1a covers the example; files with several interacting rules will need §1b.
- The independent checker itself and its runtime load-time call site (#9) — small by design, but it is *the* trusted component and must be built to a higher review standard, and the current MVP plan has it retracted.
- Certificate provenance threading — widening the engine's internal fact records with source spans at collection time (priced in `certificate-steps-membership-2026-07-12.md` § Architecture Grounding, "Provenance threading (stated build obligation)").

**Research-grade**: **nothing.** No component requires a proof technique that did not exist before; the newest load-bearing idea (checkable certificate formats at scale, DRAT) is from the 2010s, the rest is 1826–1990s. The genuinely novel part is *product* novelty — applying proof-carrying-code discipline to a domain DSL runtime with a domain-expert-legible certificate vocabulary — and its risk is design taste and completeness tuning, not mathematics.

**Performance**: not a constraint. Full 77-sample corpus compiles in ~44 ms, worst single file ~1.23 ms, and both linear-proof builds add microseconds per obligation (fresh HEAD measurements recorded in `proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` § 4; consistent with standing project evidence). The finite obligation set (events × rules × write sites) grows quadratically in file size at worst, against ~40× single-file headroom.

**Can one person with AI agents build it?** The evidence says yes, with the caveat that the estimate is grounded in this repo's own delivery record rather than external benchmarks: the existing proof engine — 7,815 LOC across 10 `ProofEngine.*` files (measured `wc -l`, 2026-07-19; the task brief's ~7,962/11-file figure is close but counts differently — `ProofLedger.cs` at 147 LOC brings the measured total to 7,962 across 11 files) — plus the graph analyzer, type checker, and ~3,600 tests were built under exactly this working model. The remaining machinery is of comparable total size (rough order: Slice 0 certificate work + §1a + obligation generation ≈ one more proof-engine's worth of code and tests, with §1b on top if the owner authorizes it). Everything is specified to the level where work can be delegated in slices; the two items that concentrate risk (the checker's trustworthiness, §1b's soundness discipline) are small enough to hold to adversarial review. The bottleneck is owner decision throughput on the gated items (spec:256 override, spec:225 ratification, checker-in-MVP vs retracted, structural severities), not engineering capacity.

**Consequences of the two open questions** (not resolved here, per the want doc):

- *Time-referencing rules.* A rule like `ExpiryDate > today` has no place in the induction — no event writes `today`, so there is no inductive step covering the passage of time, and any "rules are proven theorems" claim would be false the moment the clock passes the boundary. Feasibility-wise: if such rules are admitted as rules, the proof shape in this report does not cover them (they would need a different mechanism entirely); if time belongs only in guards, nothing in this report changes — a guard is evaluated at event time like any other premise. The machinery table above is built on the second reading and is unaffected by the choice only in that case.
- *Cross-precept promise.* Out of MVP scope by the want doc's own boundary (§ The proof boundary is the single precept). Nothing in the single-precept machinery forecloses it, but nothing here should be read as evidence about its feasibility either.

### 2.1 A caution on what "accepted" means

An accepted file demonstrates nothing by itself — the want doc says so (§ What must not compile: "the compiler's power is visible only in what it refuses"). The baseline acceptance below is **not** evidence the compiler proves the example; the mutation rows show it currently accepts most of the broken variants too.

### 2.4 The score

**2 of 11** mutations are rejected today with a diagnostic matching the table's substance (6, 8). **2 of 11** are detected with the right message but as non-blocking warnings (9, 10). **7 of 11** compile completely clean (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 11).

## Bottom line

**Can we build this?** Yes. Every required proof technique is decades-old, decidable on this surface (loop-free, function-free, finitely many events and fields), and has direct industrial precedent (Event-B's per-event invariant-preservation obligations, SPARK/Liquid-Haskell linear-arithmetic discharge, proof-carrying code + DRAT-style checkable certificates). Nothing is research-grade.

**One person with AI agents?** Yes, on the evidence of this repo itself: the ~7,900-LOC proof engine and ~3,600 tests were built that way, the remaining work is of comparable order and is specified to delegation-ready detail, and performance has ~40× headroom. The concentrated risks are the checker (small, trusted, must survive adversarial review) and §1b's soundness discipline — both bounded.

**The honest costs**: (1) the compiler today accepts 7 of the 11 files the want says must not compile — the rule-obligation machinery is the whole distance, not a refinement; (2) general rule preservation likely forces the §1b fact-combination build with its two owner-gated spec overrides (`precept-language-spec.md:256` and `:225`); (3) the current MVP plan retracted the independent checker the want doc makes load-bearing at two call sites — that retraction and the want are in direct tension and need an owner ruling; (4) prove-or-reject on a bounded prover means some true-but-hard files get rejected and asked for premises — the want doc accepts this trade explicitly, but the friction level is a tuning burden the build must carry.
