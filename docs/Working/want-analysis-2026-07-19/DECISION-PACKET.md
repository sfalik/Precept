### 2. Feasibility — "can we build this? one person with AI Agents? Does it require any new fancy proofs that didn't exist before"

**Yes; yes; no.** (`feasibility.md`, headline claims confirmed.)

- **No new proof theory.** Every obligation class falls in decidable quantifier-free linear arithmetic plus sign reasoning on a loop-free, function-free, non-recursive surface, with direct precedent: Event-B per-event invariant-preservation obligations, SPARK/Liquid-Haskell linear discharge, proof-carrying code, DRAT-style checkable certificates (`feasibility.md` § 1.2, confirmed; precedent grounded in `proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` § 2 and `certificate-steps-membership-2026-07-12.md` § Language Design Grounding). Nothing is research-grade; the novelty is product novelty.
- **The measured distance is the rule-obligation machinery, essentially in its entirety.** Live probes (re-run under verification): the compiler today accepts **7 of the 11** mutations the want says must not compile (rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 11 — clean, zero diagnostics); 2 are detected as warnings with `success:true` (rows 9, 10 — flip already ruled, unbuilt); only 2 are rejected today (rows 6, 8) (`feasibility.md` § 2.3–2.4, every row independently reproduced and confirmed). The baseline worked example mints only 3 obligations, none referencing the rule (confirmed). Mutation 7 additionally exposed a live fail-open obligation-generation hole independent of rule machinery (confirmed).
- **Certificate machinery is fully designed, zero code**: 21 step kinds, 11 premise kinds, per-step bounded recompute rules; grep for `ProofVerdict|Certificate` over `src/` returns nothing (confirmed; one parked design question on step membership noted in `certificate-steps-membership-2026-07-12.md:305-325`).
- **Performance is not a constraint**: full 77-sample corpus ~44 ms, worst file ~1.23 ms, ~40× headroom; linear-proof additions cost microseconds per obligation (confirmed).
- **One person + AI agents: yes**, on this repo's own delivery record — the 7,962-LOC proof engine (11 files) and ~3,600+ tests were built under this model; the remaining machinery is of comparable total order and specified to delegation-ready detail. The bottleneck is owner decision throughput on the gated items (spec:256, spec:225, checker scheduling, structural severities), not engineering capacity (confirmed; verification found the test count understates — 4,059 test attributes, 6,933 runner cases — an error in the claim's favor).

**Model-completion rulings** (these block the canonical doc's completeness):

**Q1 — Multi-write handler semantics: per-plan or per-write?**
*Question:* In a handler with several `set` actions, does each rule's preservation obligation quantify over the whole handler (pre-state → post-state; intermediates unobserved) or over each individual write (intermediates must also satisfy the rules)?
*Options:* (i) per-plan; (ii) per-write.
*Evidence:* the want supports both readings — "Every handler *preserves* every rule" (`what-i-want-2026-07-16.md:19`, per-plan) vs "obligations attach to every write site" (`:20`, per-write) — and nothing in it or the ledger adjudicates; the shipped PRE0078 check proves the per-write property, flagged as "stronger than the constraint's defined meaning" (`fable-analysis-of-frank-response-2026-07-11.md:98` via collation F12); the ruling also decides what survives of working-copy atomicity and the fate of `ConstraintsFailed` (`clarity-dispute-closure.md` § 1c F12(a), confirmed).

**Q2 — Checker coverage root.**
*Question:* How does the trusted side know the certificate covers *every* obligation the definition demands, not just that each recorded step replays?
*Options:* (i) the checker enumerates obligations from the definition itself (the PCC consumer-side move; enumeration enters the trusted component); (ii) coverage is assured by a compiler/build-side catalog-driven audit (the survey's C3 proposal) and the load-gate claim is narrowed accordingly ("no *wrongly-proven* definition can govern").
*Evidence:* step-walking cannot detect a never-emitted obligation; "witness-checking does NOT address generation-completeness" (`witness-checking-soundness-architecture-survey.md:172-176`); the want's claim "no unproven … definition can ever govern" (`what-i-want-2026-07-16.md:192`) requires one of the two (`soundness-uniqueness-value-philosophy.md` S2, confirmed).

**Q3 — Restore and out-of-envelope trust boundary.**
*Question:* What is the stated behavior for data the induction never covered — persisted under an older/different definition, or written out-of-band (raw SQL, migration, another service)?
*Options:* (i) bind each persisted `Version` to the identity of the governing definition/certificate and refuse or route to the post-MVP migration path on mismatch; (ii) state the guarantee's scope honestly as a declared boundary assumption (behavior outside the envelope undefined); (iii) retain a named backstop for restored data (a deliberate exception to no-post-mutation-re-evaluation).
*Evidence:* canon's justification for trust ("re-governed by the next operation's post-mutation constraint sweep," `runtime-api.md:266`) is a mechanism the want removes (`what-i-want-2026-07-16.md:153, :187`), leaving restored-stale data able to falsify premise (d) silently (`soundness-uniqueness-value-philosophy.md` S1, confirmed); spec:272's backstops for host-injected values are likewise removed, leaving tampered data with no stated behavior at all (`clarity-dispute-closure.md` Part 4 item 7, confirmed).

**Q4 — Runtime outcome and fault taxonomy.**
*Question:* What happens to the runtime API surface the want makes moot or unreachable — `ConstraintsFailed` (presupposes the sweep), the `[StaticallyPreventable]` registry rows for OutOfRange/Length/Count, and the `Faulted` variant's evaluator-internal impossible paths (undeclared field, ambiguous dispatch — not value checks)?
*Options:* per element — remove; re-scope (e.g., `ConstraintsFailed` survives only as the editable-write ingress refusal); retain the impossible-path programmer-error trap as an API shape.
*Evidence:* the want never names any of these (exhaustive grep, confirmed); the canon report explicitly flags the impossible-path question for the owner rather than resolving it (`canon-corrections-and-supersession.md` § 2.3 boundary note); disposition is behaviorally settled (both families reject when unproven) but the taxonomy is not (`clarity-dispute-closure.md` D6, confirmed).

**Q5 — Ensure membership and attachment.**
*Question:* Are `ensure` constructs (state-scoped `in`/`to`, event-anchored) rule spellings under modifiers-are-sugar, and does the obligation-attachment rule extend to constraint-*activation* sites (a no-write transition into a state whose ensure the unchanged data violates)?
*Options:* (i) yes to both — attachment extends to every row whose post-state activates a constraint, and every inbound row/entry hook is an establishment site for the target state's ensures; (ii) some narrower membership/attachment the owner prefers.
*Evidence:* the want never mentions `ensure` (confirmed by exhaustive grep); the `Freeze` + `in Frozen ensure Balance >= 0` counterexample leaks under write-site-only attachment (`soundness-uniqueness-value-philosophy.md` S3, confirmed); 64/77 samples use state-scoped ensures and the philosophy names ensures first-class (`philosophy.md:15`) (`comprehensiveness.md` M4, confirmed with corrected count).
