# Should cells live in the catalog?

**Short answer: No for the cells; yes — later, at design time — for the generative layer underneath them. The matrix's own architecture already separates "the definition" (generative sentences) from "the cells" (exhaustive coverage + witnesses), and the two belong in different homes. Recommended shape: a specific variant of D — generative rules → C# catalog when the rule-obligation machinery is designed; cells → structured data files now, permanently, with generated prose renderings and a build-time cross-check gate.**

## The decisive observation: the matrix already answered the authority question, and it dictates the storage split

The matrix (rev 3) is explicit that cells are not the definition:

- "the generative content lives in this document — the Vocabulary, the case shapes, the minting rule, and each cell's schema and discharge contract are the generative sentences; the witnesses are their worked instances" (`docs/Working/obligation-discharge-matrix-2026-07-19.md:12`)
- "Generative sentences over cell assertions… This keeps the definition generative (finite rules determining unboundedly many programs), which is what licenses calling a matrix a definition at all" (`:24`)
- "Witnesses witness the definition; they are not the definition" (`:37`)

Review-2's Lens 5 locked the same direction: "the matrix supplies *coverage and worked witnesses*; every cell answer must be derivable from a generative sentence" (`review-2-definition-fitness.md:79`). And review-3's field lesson is the sharpest precedent on record: "Rodin avoids drift because obligations are *generated from the model by rules*, not hand-enumerated in a document" (`review-3-field-precedent.md:13`), plus the Test262 lesson that hand-maintained enumeration anchors rot (`:46`).

So the question "should cells live in the catalog?" decomposes:

1. **The generative layer** — obligation-family kinds, premise classes, write-site categories, rule-structure classification, discharge contracts, step kinds, strategies, normalization rules. This IS language surface under the catalog test ("would it appear in a complete description of Precept?" — `catalog-system.md:210`; ProofRequirements is already catalog #11, `catalog-system.md:139`). The matrix itself already schedules this: rule-establishment/preservation "catalog entries are to be added when the rule-obligation machinery is designed" (`matrix:29`), and the rule-structure axis's "anchor should eventually be the expression-shape classification the rule-obligation design produces" (`matrix:70`). `CertificateStepKind` exists today only in a Working doc — no `src` grep hit — another entry on the same to-catalog list. **Catalog: yes, and it's already planned.**

2. **The cell population** — hundreds of instances of (axes × generative rules), each carrying witnesses, near-misses, respellability verdicts, dispositions, and citations. This is *derived closure plus evidence*, not dispatch metadata. The pipeline will never look up "cell (preservation × single-field × handler-set × reads-args × decimal)" at compile time — it mints obligations at sites and discharges via strategy/contract metadata. Compiling the cells into `src/Precept/Language/` would create a hand-maintained parallel copy of what the generative catalogs + engine derive — the exact artifact "derive, never duplicate" (`catalog-system.md:227-229`) forbids. Functionally, the cell population is a **conformance suite and coverage instrument** (Test262/ACATS-shaped, per review-3), and no conformance suite in the field lives inside the compiler's compiled metadata.

## Why option A (cells in the C# catalog) fails on this repo's own rules — beyond the duplication point

- **The transient-citation rule structurally excludes pre-ratification cells from `src`.** Every defined cell must "cite the want-doc sentence(s)… or the owner ruling" (`matrix:25`), dispositions cite ⧖ Q-items and packet items (`matrix:126-129`), and conflicted cells cite two canon sources. CLAUDE.md § Transient vs Canonical bans exactly these references from code: `docs/Working/` paths, Decision numbers, finding IDs, dates. A cell record without its citation violates the matrix's citation duty; with it, it violates the code-comment canon rule. Data files in the docs tree carry citations legitimately.
- **⧖-instability makes compiled cells churn.** "A change to any ⧖ part changes which cells exist" (`matrix:119`) — Q1 (write-plan decomposition) literally changes cell identity; Q5/Q6/Q10/Q13/S4 and the class-(e) ruling reshape axes. Encoding the cell product as C# kind-enums before those rulings freezes open questions into compiled shape, and re-shaping hundreds of record initializers on each ruling is the worst-cost path. It would also collide with the Pre-Design gate: two axes are marked "prose taxonomy — catalog anchor pending" *by owner-adopted review amendment* (review-3 F3; `matrix:68-70`) precisely because their classification is a design output not yet produced.
- **A `src` catalog signals implemented status.** The catalog-system doc's status is "Implemented — all catalogs in src/Precept/" (`catalog-system.md:10`). Cells will be model-derived, unverifiable for months (rule-obligation minting unbuilt; the matrix's own witness table shows "Unresolved today ✓v" against "provable under want," `matrix:161-183`), and the matrix carefully separates *committed* from *built* power (`matrix:58`). Housing that in the surface whose convention means "shipped" is a status-honesty violation waiting to happen.
- **Owner reviewability.** `Diagnostics.cs` is already 1,361 lines for 164 flat-ish entries. A cell carries five components including multi-line `.precept` witnesses and near-misses (`matrix:45-54`). Hundreds of cells as C# initializers is the least owner-reviewable form on offer, and review-4 F7.1 makes ratification-at-scale the open problem (`review-4:75-83`).

**A's strongest case, stated fairly:** "Vision precedes consumers" is emphatic — "'no consumer currently uses it' is never a valid argument against cataloging something that IS the language" (`catalog-system.md:196-204`), and under exact-iff the cells determine which programs are in the language. C# would give CS8509 totality over the axis product, object-reference integrity (the `ParameterMeta` pattern), MCP propagation via `precept_proofs` for free, and one compiled source of truth. This is a real pull, and it's why the *generative layer* does go to the catalog. But the principle's own test resolves the cell question against A: once every generative catalog's `All` is enumerated, cell answers are derivable — enumerating them again in the catalog is mirroring, not describing. The catalog owns the rules; a checker owns verifying the closure.

## Why option C (authored markdown as the artifact) fails

Review-4 F7.3 already names it: "both tracked item 4 (maintenance checker) and the 'converts mechanically into tests' claim (`matrix:221`) actually require cells to be machine-readable" (`review-4:79`). Grep/parse-the-prose at hundreds of cells is precisely the artifact class the 2026-07-14 probe post-mortem discredited (447 untrustworthy verdicts from prose-derived measurement — project memory, cited by review-4 F6/F7). Markdown also can't enforce disposition totality, citation presence, near-miss pairing, or axis-value validity — the checks that make "no silent cells" mechanical rather than aspirational. C's strongest case — the owner reads markdown, the whole doc culture is markdown, no tooling to build — is answered by generating the markdown rather than authoring it.

## Why pure B (data forever, no catalog ever) is also wrong

The discharge contracts, suggestion schemas, and step-kind semantics are exactly what Architectural Violation Pattern F says must not stay outside the catalog: "Proof discharge, diagnostic mapping, or guard reasoning encodes… semantics in handwritten switches → add proof metadata to the relevant catalog… the discharge engine becomes generic" (`catalog-system.md:242`). When the rule-obligation engine is built, its generative inputs (obligation kinds, premise classes, contracts, step kinds, suggestion templates) must be catalog metadata or the engine violates the non-negotiable. Data-forever would leave the definition's load-bearing rules outside the machine-readable spec — and create a second standing restatement once the engine encodes them, which is canon-asserts-both-answers 2.0 (review-2 F6; review-4 F8).

## Recommendation (concrete)

**Now — before population starts:**

1. **Cells as structured data files**, one file per family/case-shape, in a folder owned by the matrix (in `docs/Working/` during drafting; promoted with the matrix to a canonical `docs/` location at ratification, since `docs/Working/` is itself transient). In-repo precedent exists and is directly on point: `docs/Working/exhaustive-gap-analysis-2026-07-14/assembled/*.cells.json` — per-unit JSON cell records with `id`/`exercise`/`specCite`/`expected` fields. Reuse that shape's lessons (stable cell IDs as citation anchors — the Test262 `esid` lesson from review-3 C1 — plus the salvage discipline: verdicts from `precept_compile` codes, never prose). Per-family files bound merge conflicts and enable the staged, per-family ratification review-4 F7.1 needs.
2. **The matrix markdown remains the authored definition** — Vocabulary, case shapes, minting rule, discharge contracts as generative sentences. It is what the owner reads and ratifies. Per-family human-readable cell tables are **generated** from the data under the tmLanguage discipline: never hand-edited, drift-checked by a test (precedent: `tools/Precept.GrammarGen/` + `test/Precept.Tests/Language/TextMateGrammarTests.cs`).
3. **A cell validator from day one** (script or test, `DiagnosticCoverageScanner`-shaped completeness gate): disposition totality over the family-indexed product; citation present per cell; axis values drawn from named vocabularies — cross-checked against the built catalogs where anchors exist today (`TypeKind`, `ActionKind`, `ProofRequirementKind`, `ProofStrategy` at `ProofLedger.cs:100`) and against the matrix's declared prose taxonomies where pending; one near-miss per discharge addition; respellability verdict present; WP recomputation where the F7.2 mechanized WP calculator can reach.

**At rule-obligation design time (the trigger the matrix already names, `:29`/`:70`):**

4. **The generative layer enters the C# catalogs** — rule-establishment/preservation `ProofRequirement` subtypes, the premise-class vocabulary, `CertificateStepKind`/`CertificatePremise`, the expression-shape (rule-structure) classification, discharge-contract and suggestion-schema metadata. This is catalog-before-*code*, honored at the moment the design exists — not catalog-before-*design*, which the Pre-Design gate forbids. `precept_proofs` and the doc surfaces then propagate automatically. The matrix's generative prose re-scopes to cite the catalog rather than restate it — the same discipline `catalog-system.md` applies to itself ("this document deliberately does not enumerate catalog members," `:704`).
5. **Cell data re-anchors** its axis fields to the new enums; the validator upgrades from string-vocabulary checks to catalog cross-checks; the mechanical cell→test conversion (`matrix:221`) reads the data files.

**Permanently:** cells stay data. They are the conformance/coverage instrument and the witness record; the compiled truth is the generative catalogs; the bridge is the gate that checks catalogs ↔ cells ↔ engine behavior. Ratification and versioning ride on the data files: git history, a definition-version stamp in the data for certificate coupling, soundness-shrink amendments (review-4 F1) as owner-signed data diffs with the witness program attached.

## Tensions accepted (not resolved away)

- **Two artifacts during the interim.** Until the generative catalog entries land, contracts live only in matrix prose while cells-as-data instantiate them — a derivation gap nothing machine-checks. Mitigation is partial (the WP calculator, citation checks); the residual risk is exactly review-4 F2/F6's model-derived-at-scale warning, and it exists under every storage shape. Data doesn't fix it; it only makes the eventual check buildable.
- **Generated prose ≠ reviewed source.** The owner ratifies data he reads through a rendering. The ratification protocol (review-4 F7.1's one-page note) must define what the owner actually reads — generative sections in full, generated family tables, machine-check results, spot-check contract. That note is still the genuine prerequisite before population, whatever the format.
- **Deferring the catalog entries defers the "single atomic act" propagation** — MCP/hover/docs won't know rule-obligation kinds until the design pass. That is the price of not cataloging shapes that are ⧖-gated on owner rulings; I judge it correct under the Pre-Design gate, but it is a real deviation from the "vision precedes consumers" maximal reading, and an owner who wants the *family kinds alone* (just the enum members, no contracts) cataloged earlier could defensibly order that without waiting for the full design.
- **JSON vs YAML** is left to the population-plan note; the repo precedent is JSON (`*.cells.json`), which diffs acceptably if formatted one-field-per-line; multi-line `.precept` witnesses read better in YAML block scalars. Either satisfies every criterion above; deciding it is part of F7.1's one-pager, not this ruling.

**Bottom line:** "Should cells live in the catalog?" — the catalog should own the *rules that generate the cells*, on the timeline the matrix already sets; the cells themselves should be machine-checked data beside the matrix, rendered to prose for the owner, and never compiled into `src` — because in this architecture the cells are not the definition, and putting derived closure in the catalog is the parallel-copy failure the catalog system exists to prevent.
