## 1. The maximal legible engine, derived from first principles

### 1a. Legibility is a property of the certificate, not the search

spec:225 currently states the legibility commitment as a tool exclusion ("SMT/Z3 solvers are excluded"). That conflates two things the proof-theory tradition keeps separate: **how a verdict is found** and **what evidence ships with it**. The correct line — the de Bruijn criterion from proof assistants (de Bruijn/Automath, 1970s; the LCF architecture, Milner 1979; proof-carrying code, Necula & Lee 1997) — is:

> A decision procedure is admissible iff every verdict it emits ships with a certificate drawn from a small, closed, spec-enumerated vocabulary of certificate forms, where each form (a) replays in an independent, tiny checker against the runtime's own semantics, and (b) renders as a bounded-size, domain-vocabulary explanation naming the author's own declarations.

Under this criterion the prover's internals are unconstrained — it may even use an SMT solver as a *search oracle* — but a verdict without a legible certificate is treated as *no verdict*. This is strictly stronger than spec:225 as a principle (today's interval engine is not currently held to certificate-replay) and strictly more powerful in coverage.

**Recommended spec change (replaces spec:225's second half):** "Proof verdicts are certificate-defined: no verdict ships without a replayable, domain-renderable certificate from the spec-enumerated certificate vocabulary. Search may use any procedure; authority rests only in the certificate. Certificates exceeding the spec-fixed legibility budget are treated as unproven." (The budget must be in the spec, not the implementation — determinism requires same-definition-same-verdict across compiler versions.)

### 1b. The legible certificate vocabulary

Each of these is inspectable by a domain expert because each step names an authored declaration and applies one teachable rule:

1. **Interval derivation** — "Price is between 0 and 100 (its declared band); Qty between 1 and 50; so Price×Qty is between 0 and 5,000." (Already spec'd, §0.6 #1.)
2. **Named-premise linear combination (Farkas witness)** — "Add rule 'Deposits ≥ Withdrawals' to the declared bound 'Fees ≤ 10': Deposits − Withdrawals − Fees ≥ −10." Farkas' lemma (1902) guarantees every true linear entailment has such a witness; Fourier–Motzkin (1826/1936) or simplex finds it. A 2–4 line combination with small coefficients over named rules is *more* legible than today's depth-bounded relational special case.
3. **Concrete counterexample configuration** — "With Deposits = '0.00 USD' and Withdrawals = '5.00 USD' (both satisfy every declared constraint), Balance = '−5.00 USD', below its min. " The single most legible artifact possible.
4. **Finite case split on named conditions** — for `if/then/else`, `min/max/abs`, disjunctive guards, calendar month lengths, small-constant residue classes ("whether N is even or odd…").
5. **Monotone endpoint mapping** — `sqrt`, `pow` with constant exponent, `floor/ceil/round` bracketing (`x−1 < floor(x) ≤ x`), place-value grid facts ("cents × whole number stays on the cent grid").
6. **Corner evaluation for multilinear expressions over declared boxes** — a bilinear/multilinear expression over independent interval premises attains its extrema at the interval corners; the certificate is the evaluated corner ("worst case: X at max 100, Y at max 50 → 5,000").
7. **Congruence discharge of an author-stated fact** — the obligation's term syntactically matches a declared rule's term: "your rule 'Width × Height ≤ 100' directly bounds this expression." The engine never *derives* the nonlinear fact; it *applies* it, and governance makes it true at every ingress.
8. **Equality substitution** — `rule Total == Subtotal + Tax` used definitionally (Gaussian elimination over declared equalities; each step names the rule).

### 1c. The structural theorem the language's austerity buys — and the spec does not yet cash in

Because the language is loop-free, branch-free-reconverging, and pure (§0.4, spec:158-174), **weakest-precondition computation is exact substitution** — Dijkstra's WP calculus (1975) is *complete* for straight-line code; the entire apparatus of loop invariants, widening, and fixpoints (the actual source of illegibility and incompleteness in every general-purpose verifier) is absent by construction. Concretely: for any action sequence, the condition "this `set F = E` lands in F's band" is a **closed-form, quantifier-free predicate in Precept's own expression language over pre-state fields and event arguments**, obtained by substituting earlier same-row assignments into E.

Two consequences, both load-bearing for the verdict:

- **The engine can always *compute and print* the exact safety condition**, even when it cannot decide it. spec:266 promises the rejection "names what would make the operation provably safe" — the maximal-legible engine makes that literal: the diagnostic hands the author the precise predicate ("this row is safe exactly when: Deposits ≥ Withdrawals"), which is paste-able as a `when` guard or declarable as a `rule`.
- **Every containment obligation is statable by the author in the language itself.** The obligation predicate is an expression of the language; guards and rules accept expressions of the language; therefore the discharging fact is always authorable. This closes the decidability-ceiling question in §4.

### 1d. The trichotomy correction — the debate's flagship example is not in the cell

With full linear entailment (certificate forms 2 and 3), the **linear fragment is decided in both directions**: for linear E and linear premises, exactly one of three holds, and the engine can always tell which — *proven in-band* (Farkas certificate), *proven always-violating* (dead row), or *proven sometimes-violating* (concrete counterexample witness). Linear rational arithmetic is decidable (Fourier–Motzkin); decimals and money embed in the rationals; "unbounded source into bounded field" is the *sometimes-violating* case with a trivial witness, **not** an unprovable case.

This matters because both position papers treat `set Balance = Deposits - Withdrawals` into `min 0` as the canonical "merely-unprovable" inhabitant (`frank-prove-or-reject-position-2026-07-11.md:22,45-53`; thesis §2.4(ii) "unbounded interval into a bounded field"). Under the right engine it is not an epistemic gap at all: the compiler *knows the exact truth* and can exhibit `Deposits='0.00', Withdrawals='5.00'`. The policy fork (reject vs. govern the sometimes-violating write) remains a real owner decision — but it is a **policy** question about a *known* truth, not an expressiveness question, and the rejection message in that cell is never "I couldn't tell"; it is "here is a valid configuration that breaks it," which transforms prove-or-reject's legibility economics. Frank's "merely unprovable … undecidable-in-general territory" framing (frank-position:22) is only reachable in the nonlinear residue below.

## 2. The classification table

"FUNDAMENTAL" = no engine anyone can build decides it silently, or the only deciding procedures emit certificates illegible to this audience. "SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE" = the maximal legible engine handles it; the current spec forecloses it.

| # | Computation class | Classification | Recommended spec change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Linear arithmetic (`+`, `−`, constant `×`) over integer/decimal/money, with relational **and** interval premises, any number of fields | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Replace spec:256's "single-pass and depth-bounded (no transitive chasing of a third field)" with full linear entailment over the declared premise set: Fourier–Motzkin/simplex search, Farkas-combination certificates for proofs, concrete-witness certificates for refutations. Decidable both directions; **the merely-unprovable cell for this class is empty.** Termination/perf is a non-issue at Precept scale (handfuls of fields, ~44ms full-corpus compiles). |
| 2 | Piecewise & monotone builtins: `min/max/abs`, `if/then/else`, `floor/ceil/round/truncate`, `sqrt`, `pow` with constant exponent | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Case-split compilation (each `min/max/abs`/conditional node splits on its named condition) + monotone endpoint mapping + rounding-bracket identities. Reduces to class 1 per case. Spec should enumerate these certificate forms in §0.6. |
| 3 | Multilinear expressions (`X*Y`, `X/Y`, `X*X`) over **independent interval premises** (boxes) | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Corner-evaluation certificates (exact for multilinear over boxes), sign rules, square-on-sign-split. Interval product already partially spec'd; make exactness and the corner witness explicit. |
| 4 | Equality-defined derived quantities (`rule Total == Subtotal + Tax`) | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Admit equality rules as substitution premises (spec:256 currently lists only `>=,>,<=,<`). Pure legibility win; linear equalities keep class 1 decidable. |
| 5 | Author-stated **nonlinear** facts as premises (`rule X * Y <= Capacity`) | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Congruence discharge: match the obligation's term against the declared rule's term; governance enforces the rule at ingress, so the premise is structurally true of committed state. (Sequencing caveat: a mid-plan write to X invalidates the premise for later same-plan reads — the existing sequential-flow machinery, spec item 7, already owns this.) |
| 6 | Aggregates: `sum`, `min`, `max`, `average` over collections with declared `maxcount` and element bounds | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** (collection-types.md:860) | Admit named numeric aggregates. The proof calculus is the most domain-legible in the entire engine: `sum ∈ [count·min-element, count·max-element]` — "at most 100 line items × at most $50 each = at most $5,000" is *exactly* how the rule-owner already reasons. `average` inherits the existing `count > 0` obligation machinery. Unbounded count into a bounded field → class-1 witness ("40 items of $50 exceeds your $1,000 cap"), not an unprovable. See §3(ii) for the reduce line. |
| 7 | Quantified element facts as premises (`each a in Amounts (a >= 0)` ⊢ element bounds) | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Element-bound transfer into aggregate/accessor obligations; the inner-type-bound precedent (spec's `maxlength` carrier on collection accessors) already establishes the pattern. |
| 8 | Constant-modulus congruence (`X % 4`, parity, divisibility by a literal) | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Residue-class case-split certificates for small spec-budgeted moduli; range fact `X % k ∈ [0, k−1]` unconditionally. |
| 9 | String-length arithmetic (concat, interpolation, length-stable functions) | Already handled (spec:243-244) — linear, decidable | Fold under class 1's certificate regime. |
| 10 | Calendar/temporal arithmetic (month-add irregularity, day-length bounds) | **SPEC-SHOULD-CHANGE** | Finite case-split over calendar bounds (month length ∈ [28,31], etc.); discrete-linear underneath. |
| 11 | **Nonlinear terms interacting with relational premises** — products/quotients of related variables, variable modulus, variable exponent, where neither a box (class 3) nor a matching authored fact (class 5) closes it: e.g. `set F = X*Y` given only `rule X >= Y`, band on F | **FUNDAMENTAL** | Over integers/exact decimals: undecidable for *any* engine — Hilbert's 10th problem (Matiyasevich 1970); nonlinear integer entailment admits no algorithm. Over reals: decidable (Tarski 1951) but only via CAD (Collins 1975) or Positivstellensatz/sum-of-squares (Parrilo 2003) whose certificates — cell decompositions, high-degree SOS multipliers — fail the domain-expert renderability test regardless of budget. Both prongs of the cell definition (genuinely undecidable; provable-only-illegibly) land here and only here. **Mitigation, not proof:** the WP-verbatim named-fix (§1c) plus classes 3/5 shrink the *practical* residue to near zero. |
| 12 | General `reduce`/`map`/`filter` with author-supplied combining logic | **FUNDAMENTAL — and correctly excluded** (keep this half of collection-types.md:860) | A fold is a loop in disguise: its WP requires an inductive invariant, precisely the reasoning §0.4 excludes and the one thing no legible certificate vocabulary covers. The line to draw is *named algebraic aggregates in, user-defined folds out* — not "all aggregation out." |

## 3. The three re-opened design questions

**(i) Derived-value invariant: re-proved obligation (spec:266) or checkable premise?** Re-open honestly: the *assume-with-obligation* posture (band assumed for downstream reasoning, checked at runtime) is the GOVERN design, off the table here; but even inside prove-or-reject there is a real fork about what "prove" costs. The analysis above resolves it on cost grounds: **keep the re-proved obligation, because under the maximal legible engine its price collapses.** In the linear fragment (the overwhelming business majority — the corpus's money flows are sums/differences) the obligation is *decided*, never merely-unprovable, and rejection carries a concrete witness. In the nonlinear residue, exact WP means the rejection hands the author the verbatim discharging predicate. The one spec amendment required: spec:266's "names what would make the operation provably safe" should be strengthened from an aspiration to a defined artifact — *the rejection must include the computed weakest precondition and the trichotomy verdict (in-band / always-violating / witness / undecided-nonlinear)*. A rejection that says "cannot prove" without the WP is, under this design, an engine defect. Additionally, a write proven (or entering) in-band establishes the band as a premise for *downstream* obligations in the same plan — assume-after-establish, which is just sequential proof flow (spec item 7) extended to bands.

**(ii) Should sum/reduce exist (collection-types.md:860)?** Split the held-back set. The current rationale ("would cross the line from predicate into computation") is right about `reduce`/`map`/`filter` and wrong about `sum`/`min`/`max`/`average`. Named algebraic aggregates have closed-form, monotone bound-transfer rules (table row 6) — they *extend* the interval calculus rather than escaping it, and their certificates are the most domain-natural in the product. Withholding `sum` from a language whose target author governs invoices, claims, and ledgers is a severe expressiveness gap with no proof-engine justification — the proof story is the *easy* part. General folds stay out on the fundamental ground of row 12 (invariant-requiring, hence illegible), which is a *stronger* justification than the current "larger feature surface" line. Recommended change: collection-types.md admits the four named aggregates over numeric-element collections, with bound-transfer proof rules and the `count > 0` obligation for `average`; the exclusion sentence is rewritten to exclude *user-defined combining logic* specifically, citing the invariant argument.

**(iii) Where does legibility draw the solver line? Do certificate-producing decision procedures qualify?** **Yes — that is the line.** Not "no SMT" (a tool name), not "intervals plus one relational special case" (the current engine's shape), but the certificate criterion of §1a: closed certificate vocabulary, independent replay, domain rendering, spec-fixed budget. Fourier–Motzkin with Farkas witnesses qualifies (each step is "combine these two named rules"). Corner evaluation qualifies. Residue splits with small moduli qualify. CAD and sum-of-squares do *not* — not because they are solvers, but because their certificates cannot be rendered in domain vocabulary at bounded size; they fail on the criterion's own terms, which is exactly the reassurance that the criterion, not taste, is doing the work. One honest tension to pin in the spec: a legibility *budget* makes provability at the margin depend on a spec constant; determinism (`philosophy.md:22`) requires that constant be part of the language definition, versioned like any other semantic — never an implementation dial.

## 4. Verdict

**What is genuinely in the cell, after the right design?** Only table row 11: containment questions where nonlinear terms interact with relational premises over exact domains, with no matching authored fact and no box structure — Diophantine-shaped entailments. This is a fundamental wall for *every* compiler ever buildable (Matiyasevich), and over the reals a legibility wall (Tarski-decidable only through certificates no domain expert can read). Nothing else survives: every case both position papers actually argue over — unbounded money differences, sums into caps, the 214-unbounded-field corpus — is **decided with a legible artifact** (Farkas proof or concrete counterexample) by an engine the current spec forecloses at spec:256 and collection-types.md:860. The "merely-unprovable" cell as the debate has been using it is, in the linear world, **empty** — and the debate should be re-fought with that fact on the table, since the thesis's over-rejection cost and Frank's "undecidable-in-general territory" both dissolve for the flagship examples.

**The decidability ceiling: is there a hard wall no legible design closes?** For *silent, unaided proof*: yes — row 11, and it is a wall for anyone, not a Precept limitation. For *the author's ability to state every safety fact as something checked*: **no wall exists.** Because the language is loop-free and pure, every containment obligation reduces by exact substitution to a quantifier-free predicate *of the language itself*, which the author can always declare — as a `rule` (a checked premise, made structurally true by governance at every ingress, consumed by congruence discharge) or as a row `when` guard (which discharges the obligation by matching it exactly, and forces the complement case to an explicit authored disposition — reject row, no-transition, alternate computation). The engine can even *print* that predicate in the rejection. Pure prove-or-reject with no escape construct is therefore **expressively closed**: the residual authoring cost in the fundamental cell is never "invent a fact you can't state to appease the prover" — it is precisely "answer the domain question your definition left open," stated back to you verbatim. The single genuine expressiveness loss of the no-escape posture is the author who wants "compute it and let the value land where it lands" with no stated disposition for the out-of-band case — and refusing that author a spelling is not a limit of the engine; it is the prevention identity doing exactly what it claims.

**Dependency disclosure:** this verdict's "no wall on statability" leg depends on two held-fixed identity commitments (loop-free purity, which makes WP exact; one-file completeness, which makes governance able to enforce authored premises) and on four challenged spec decisions being changed as recommended (full linear entailment replacing spec:256's depth bound; WP-verbatim rejections strengthening spec:266; congruence/equality premises; named aggregates replacing half of collection-types.md:860). If the spec changes are *not* adopted, the cell balloons to include ordinary linear business arithmetic — and the prove-or-reject debate is then being fought over an artificially weak engine, which no party should accept as the ground of an identity decision.
