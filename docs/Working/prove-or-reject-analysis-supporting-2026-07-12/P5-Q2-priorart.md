## Summary

Wrote `/home/sfalik/source/repos/Precept/research/architecture/compiler/nonlinear-undecidable-proof-escape-mechanisms-2026-07-12.md` and added its entry to `research/INDEX.md` (§ Proof engine, proof discharge).

**Core finding**: across SPARK/GNATprove, Frama-C/WP, Dafny, F*, and Liquid Haskell, "we couldn't prove it, so it's forbidden" never appears as a terminal outcome for nonlinear arithmetic. Every system resolves to the same three-tier pattern: (1) automatic proof, possibly with more solver resources; (2) **supply a decidable, checked fact** — SPARK's ghost-procedure lemma library (Yannick Moy, AdaCore blog 2016: nonlinear arithmetic fails because "the theory of arithmetic is undecidable," closed by calling e.g. `Lemma_Mult_Is_Monotonic`), Dafny's lemmas/`calc`/opaque-reveal, Frama-C/WP's ACSL lemmas, F*'s tactics/Meta-F*, Liquid Haskell's refinement-reflection ("write equational proofs of programs just by writing other programs," Vazou et al. POPL 2018) plus PLE to reduce the manual-proof burden; (3) an explicit **unsound trust escape** (`pragma Assume` / `assume` / `admit`-class) that every one of the five keeps as a real fallback.

**Adversarial findings that cut against a comfortable "known bounded tradeoff" reading**:
- Precept's own soundness principle (`precept-language-spec.md:221`, "must never claim an expression is safe when it is not") already forecloses Tier 3 — a real, named cost every comparator pays that Precept's stated principles would not permit itself.
- The five systems split on whether unprovability **gates the artifact's existence**: SPARK/Frama-C/Astrée/CBMC/Infer decouple verification from compilation (the program compiles and runs regardless of proof status); Dafny/F*/Liquid Haskell gate compilation on it. Critically, **every gating system is also SMT-based**, and every solver-free system is non-gating. No comparator combines solver-free + gating + no-trust-escape — the specific conjunction Precept's stated posture aims at (spec:225 SMT exclusion + prove-or-reject + no assume-escape) has no direct precedent, though each pairwise combination is mainstream.

**Design-ceiling classification (per the FUNDAMENTAL vs MERELY-UNIMPLEMENTED discipline)**: Precept's `rule` construct (`precept-language-spec.md:1138`, explicitly interchangeable with a constraint modifier as a proof premise) already occupies the field's Tier-2 "supply the missing fact" slot for the existing linear/relational fragment, and the catalog-sourced `DynamicObligationGenerator` channel (`type-proof-stage-contract-survey.md:56,120`) is architecturally the same shape SPARK/Dafny use for lemma discharge (a bounded structural check, not algebra). Extending it to nonlinear (product/ratio) premises is therefore **MERELY-UNIMPLEMENTED**, not fundamentally blocked — genuine fundamental limits are narrower (integer nonlinear arithmetic is undecidable per Hilbert's Tenth Problem/Matiyasevich; real nonlinear arithmetic is decidable but only via quantifier-elimination machinery that's principle-excluded, not fundamentally impossible — both flagged Tertiary, non-load-bearing).

Also found: Precept structurally has **no** unbounded-accumulation or transcendental-function surface today (no loops per spec:158, no `sum`/`total`/`average` accessor per `collection-types.md:85-95`), so it sidesteps the corpus's single largest escape-mechanism cost (loop-invariant/widening annotation burden) by construction — flagged as a genuine point in Precept's favor, not invented as a design proposal (adding such surface would trigger the CLAUDE.md language-surface consultation gate, out of scope here).
