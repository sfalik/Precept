# Prior Art: How Sound Verifiers Handle Nonlinear and Undecidable Computation — Reject, or Supply-the-Missing-Fact?

> This is Q2 of a multi-question research effort grounding Precept's proof-engine posture on nonlinear/undecidable computation. It asks a narrower question than the corpus already answers: not "bounds vs. values" or "who generates obligations," but *what actually happens, mechanically and ergonomically, the moment auto-proof fails* — and whether "we couldn't prove it, so it's forbidden" is ever a real comparator outcome, or whether the field's universal answer is "supply the missing fact."

## Background

This survey feeds the still-open, owner-ungated conversation about Precept's proof-engine boundary captured in three Draft, non-binding agent artifacts — `docs/Working/precept-identity-and-guarantees-thesis-2026-07-11.md`, `docs/Working/frank-prove-or-reject-position-2026-07-11.md`, `docs/Working/fable-analysis-of-frank-response-2026-07-11.md` — all self-labeled "Draft … pending Shane ratification" and none of which bind this research. Per those documents, the live disagreement is about *declared-band containment on merely-unprovable derived values*; this Q2 asks the adjacent, more general question the design conversation needs answered from outside Precept: across the field's sound static verifiers, when a computation is nonlinear (multiplication/division of two non-constant quantities) or otherwise not decidable by the tool's automatic strategy, is expressiveness *lost* (the computation becomes unwritable) or *preserved* through an author-supplied mechanism, and at what cost?

This builds forward from three files already in the repo's corpus without duplicating them:
- `research/architecture/compiler/interval-vs-value-evaluation-prior-art-2026-06-05.md` — the abstraction-vs-evaluation axis; already documents SPARK's Interval pass, Liquid Haskell's decidable-fragment discipline, and the "supply-a-precondition" posture at a general level.
- `research/architecture/compiler/type-proof-stage-contract-survey.md` — who generates obligations and how they cross the type-checker↔verifier boundary; already documents Whiley/Dafny/Frama-C/SPARK/F*/Viper/Why3 mechanics.
- `research/architecture/compiler/proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md` — raw per-system mechanics (SPARK, Frama-C EVA, Astrée, Liquid Haskell, Dafny, CBMC, Infer): what each proves, how it represents intervals, how it handles loops, its proof-obligation classification.

None of the three asks *what the author does next* when the automatic strategy fails on a nonlinear or undecidable goal — that is this file's addition: the lemma library / ghost code / tactic / reflection escape-mechanism layer, its cost, and whether "forbidden, full stop" ever actually happens.

## Findings

### 1. The proximate cause is the same across every system: nonlinear (and worse) arithmetic is outside the automatic decision procedure's comfort zone

Every comparator gives essentially the same proximate reason, in its own vocabulary:

**SPARK/GNATprove** — Alt-Ergo's built-in theory is weak on multiplication and division of two variables:

> "the automatic prover Alt-Ergo has only a simple support for non-linear integer arithmetic" — and this is "a known difficulty for all automatic provers."
> — SPARK User's Guide § 7.8 How to Investigate Unproved Checks [Secondary/vendor doc; access date 2026-07-12; https://docs.adacore.com/spark2014-docs/html/ug/en/source/how_to_investigate_unproved_checks.html]

Yannick Moy (AdaCore), writing the canonical explanation of *why*, roots it in the underlying decision-theory boundary, not an implementation accident:

> "the theory of arithmetic is undecidable" — SMT provers handle Presburger arithmetic (addition/subtraction) well but have "much weaker support for multiplication and division (except for multiplication by an integer constant which is treated as repeated additions)."
> — Yannick Moy, "GNATprove Tips and Tricks: Using the Lemma Library," AdaCore blog, 2016-05-25 [Secondary/named-author vendor blog; access date 2026-07-12; https://www.adacore.com/blog/gnatprove-tips-and-tricks-using-the-lemma-library]

**Dafny** states the identical boundary in its own FAQ, with the identical carve-out for constant multiplication:

> "Numerical formulas involving multiplication are also undecidable in general. (Multiplication by any constant value such as `2 * x` are fine though.)"
> — Dafny GitHub wiki, FAQ [Primary/project doc; access date 2026-07-12; https://github.com/dafny-lang/dafny/wiki/FAQ]

**F\*** frames it at the SMT-theory level, generalizing beyond arithmetic to any undecidable background theory:

> "Many background theories in SMT are undecidable, including the theory of nonlinear integer arithmetic" — and "since first-order logic is undecidable, when Z3 fails to find a proof, it reports 'unknown' rather than claiming that the theory is satisfiable."
> — F* tutorial, "Understanding how F* uses Z3" [Primary/project doc; access date 2026-07-12; https://fstar-lang.org/tutorial/book/under_the_hood/uth_smt.html]

**Frama-C/WP** hits the same wall on a concrete, minimal example — the identity `(a*b)/b = a` for non-negative `a`, positive `b` — which "is readily provided by Coq, but not by WP" automatically [Secondary/arXiv preprint on a Frama-C/WP verification case study; access date 2026-07-12].

**Liquid Haskell** is the partial outlier: its Vazou/Jhala lineage documents that when *Z3* is the backend, "LiquidHaskell allows for non-linear arithmetic: division and multiplication on integers are interpreted by z3" [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12] — i.e. LH inherits whatever Z3 itself can do (best-effort, not sound-and-complete) rather than refusing nonlinear terms outright, but LH's own literature is explicit that this SMT-level support does **not** extend to properties requiring induction/equational reasoning (associativity, commutativity, monotonicity over recursive structures) — that gap is closed by a completely different mechanism (§3 below).

**Conclusion of this section:** the field's diagnosis is uniform and it is the theoretical one, not a tooling accident — nonlinear integer arithmetic is a genuinely undecidable decision problem (Matiyasevich's resolution of Hilbert's Tenth Problem: no algorithm decides solvability of an arbitrary Diophantine — i.e. integer polynomial — equation [Tertiary/general knowledge, not separately re-verified this session; flagged]), and even where a *decidable* fragment exists (nonlinear arithmetic over the *reals*, decidable in principle by Tarski–Seidenberg quantifier elimination [Tertiary/general knowledge, same flag]), the decision procedure is doubly-exponential in the worst case — nobody in this comparator set runs full real quantifier elimination as their default discharge strategy. Every comparator's automatic layer is a *fragment* of a larger, harder theory, by design, not by oversight.

### 2. What happens next: the escape-mechanism catalog, per system

**SPARK/GNATprove — three escalating tiers, ghost-procedure lemmas as the mainstream middle tier.**

The failure mode is not silence: an unproved nonlinear check is reported and classified (`Unproved`, per the raw survey's classification table already on file — `proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md:49-59`). The escape mechanism is the **SPARK lemma library**, introduced in SPARK Pro 17 specifically to close this gap:

> A library of lemmas "can be used to prove more complex properties that cannot be proved fully automatically."
> — SPARK User's Guide § 5.11 SPARK Libraries [Secondary/vendor doc; access date 2026-07-12]

Moy frames the lemma library's place explicitly as a **third point between full automation and full interactivity**:

> Lemma calls fill "an interesting point in the matrix of techniques between fully automated proof and interactive proof."
> — Moy, AdaCore blog, 2016 [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12]

Mechanically, a lemma is a **ghost procedure** — precondition = hypothesis, postcondition = conclusion — that the author *calls* at the point where the automatic prover is stuck, so the postcondition becomes an available fact:

```ada
procedure Lemma_Mult_Is_Monotonic
  (Val1   : Int;
   Val2   : Int;
   Factor : Nat)
with
  Global => null,
  Pre  => Val1 <= Val2,
  Post => Val1 * Factor <= Val2 * Factor;
```
— Moy, AdaCore blog, 2016 [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12]

Calling `Lemma_Mult_Scale` before a division "enabled the prover to verify that 'this value is between 0 and V(J)'", discharging a range check the automatic pass alone could not [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12].

If the built-in library doesn't cover the needed fact, the author writes a **custom lemma**: implement it as a null procedure body (an unproved obligation, honestly surfaced, not hidden), add `pragma Assume` (an explicit, greppable trust declaration — see §4), or write ghost code that lets the automatic prover discharge the lemma's own body. If none of that closes it, the lemma's proof is written **by hand in Coq** — the SPARK User's Guide's Manual Proof Examples page walks through exactly this [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12].

**Never a dead end.** At every tier the computation remains writable — the escalation is: automatic → call a library lemma → write a custom lemma (possibly proved manually) → Coq. Crucially, an *unproved* check in SPARK does **not** stop the Ada program from compiling or running: GNATprove is a separate analysis tool from the Ada compiler (`proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md:51-59` already documents the `Unproved`/`Justified` output buckets as *analysis classifications*, not compiler gates). Verification and compilation are decoupled in SPARK — a fact with real bearing on Precept's posture (§5).

**Dafny — lemmas, `calc`, opaque functions, and a compile-time verification gate.**

Dafny's default posture is stricter than SPARK's: by default, `dafny build`/`dafny run` require verification to pass before code generation. But the *nonlinear-specific* escape is structurally identical to SPARK's — auxiliary lemmas proved by structural induction, invoked at the point of need:

> "Dafny provides examples of lemmas building from basic properties about multiplication to properties about exponentiation, which can be proved and used with the `--disable-nonlinear-arithmetic` flag enabled. These lemmas demonstrate techniques including lots of small lemmas, opaque functions, and calc statements."
> [Secondary/search-synthesized from Dafny project docs; access date 2026-07-12]

The FAQ gives the two first-line workarounds directly: "you can sometimes work around this by breaking your formula down into smaller pieces by introducing new functions," or "turn off nonlinear arithmetic in the solver using `/noNLarith`" [Primary; access date 2026-07-12]. Beyond that: `calc` chains (step-by-step equational proof, each step separately checkable), `opaque` functions with explicit `reveal` statements to control what the solver sees (avoiding runaway quantifier instantiation on recursive/nonlinear definitions), and the general Dafny/Boogie escape hatch `assume` (proceed on an explicitly unverified fact — same category as SPARK's `pragma Assume`, see §4).

**Frama-C/WP — user-stated ACSL lemmas, discharged automatically when possible, by hand in Coq when not.**

The `(a*b)/b = a` case study is instructive precisely because it is small: the property "must be explicitly stated as a lemma" in ACSL, and "the Coq proof applies a lemma that states the same property for multiplication and division on Z" [Secondary/arXiv case study; access date 2026-07-12]. In the surveyed project, only **two** lemmas required interactive Coq proof; the rest of the nonlinear-adjacent obligations were discharged automatically once the two foundational facts were available as assumed premises [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12] — the same "small number of hand proofs unlock a much larger automatically-discharged surface" shape as SPARK's lemma library.

**F\* — SMT resource escalation, then tactics/metaprogramming, then explicit `admit`.**

F*'s escape ladder, in the order the tutorial presents it: (1) raise the SMT resource budget (`--z3rlimit_factor`, `--fuel`/`--ifuel` to control unfolding depth) — a "maybe it just needs more search" tier with no author-supplied fact yet; (2) mark definitions `[@@"opaque_to_smt"]` so their bodies don't flood the solver with irrelevant quantifiers, revealing them selectively; (3) **tactics and metaprogramming** (Meta-F*) to construct the proof term directly when SMT search alone won't converge; (4) `assume`, F*'s explicit unproved-trust declaration [Primary/project tutorial; access date 2026-07-12]. The tutorial is explicit that unproved conditions cause compilation failure, not silent pass-through, and that the responsibility "rests with the programmer to either provide stronger proofs or justify unprovable claims" [Primary; access date 2026-07-12].

**Liquid Haskell — refinement reflection: the author writes the proof as an ordinary program.**

LH's answer to "SMT-decidable refinement typing cannot verify properties requiring induction" (associativity, monotonicity, anything over a recursive structure — exactly where the nonlinear-and-relational cases in Precept's own domain corpus tend to live, per `bounds-only-constraint-enforcement-2026-06-05.md`'s 3-field-sum and product-shaped findings) is **refinement reflection**, Vazou et al.'s POPL 2018 mechanism:

> Refinement reflection lifts "the code implementing a user-defined function ... into the function's (output) refinement type," letting programmers "write equational proofs of programs just by writing other programs using pattern-matching and recursion to perform case-splitting and induction," via the propositions-as-types principle, to verify "arbitrary functional correctness properties."
> — Vazou, Tanter, Weimer, et al., "Refinement Reflection: Complete Verification with SMT," POPL 2018 (arXiv:1711.03842) [Primary/peer-reviewed; access date 2026-07-12]

The cost is stated plainly by the accompanying course material: proofs are "recursive functions in Liquid Haskell," and **Proof by Logical Evaluation (PLE)** exists specifically to reduce how much of that proof the author must spell out by hand — PLE "executes functions for as many steps as needed and automatically provides all the resulting equations to the SMT solver," turning many inductive proofs from fully-manual case-split-and-recurse code into a one-line `by` annotation [Secondary/course page; access date 2026-07-12].

### 3. The universal three-tier outcome taxonomy

Every comparator, independent of vocabulary (lemma/ghost/tactic/reflection), resolves to the same three possible outcomes when a nonlinear or otherwise undecidable-in-general goal is reached:

| Tier | Mechanism | What the author supplies | Soundness | Does the computation stay writable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Automatic, possibly with more resources** | SMT search, resource bump (`--z3rlimit_factor`, `/noNLarith` off), constant-folding for special cases | Nothing new — a bigger budget or a solver flag | Sound (if it succeeds) | Yes, unconditionally |
| **2. Supply the missing fact** | Lemma call (SPARK), lemma/calc/opaque-reveal (Dafny), ACSL lemma (Frama-C/WP), tactic/metaprogram (F*), reflection/PLE proof-as-program (Liquid Haskell) | A **decidable, checkable** fact — itself proved automatically, by a smaller lemma, or (last resort) by hand in Coq/Isabelle | Sound — the fact is *proved*, not assumed | Yes, at an author-time cost proportional to how far the goal is from the decidable fragment |
| **3. Explicit trust escape** | `pragma Assume` (SPARK), `assume` (Dafny/Boogie/F*), a `sorry`/`admit`-class marker in the interactive-proof tier | An explicitly labeled, greppable, unverified claim | **Unsound** — the tool proceeds without proof, on the author's word | Yes — the computation still compiles/runs; the *claim* is unverified, not the *program* |

**No comparator surveyed has a fourth outcome of "the tool refuses to let this computation be written at all, with no path forward."** Every failure to auto-prove routes to Tier 2 (in the overwhelming majority of documented cases) or, when Tier 2's cost is judged too high by the author, to the openly-unsound Tier 3. This directly answers the brief's central question: **"we couldn't prove it, so it's forbidden" is not the field's actual outcome for nonlinear arithmetic in any of the five surveyed systems.** The consistent answer is "supply the missing fact," with an explicit (and honestly labeled) unsound fallback when the author declines to.

### 4. The Tier-3 escape hatch is the one Precept's stated principles already exclude — and every comparator has it

This is the sharpest finding for Precept's specific situation. `pragma Assume` / `assume` / interactive-proof `admit`-class markers are a **third, distinct mechanism** from "supply a proved fact" — they let the author proceed with an explicitly-labeled but **unverified** claim. This is structurally *not* SMT (Precept's spec:225 exclusion targets solver opacity, not author-declared trust) and *not* the "governed-at-runtime" construct this research brief has been told is off the table (an `assume` is never checked again, by anything, at any time — it is weaker than runtime governance, not a variant of it). But it is flatly incompatible with Precept's own stated proof philosophy:

> "The proof layer must never claim an expression is safe when it is not." — Proof philosophy #1, Soundness over completeness.
> — `docs/language/precept-language-spec.md:221`

An `assume`-class escape is precisely a mechanism for the tool to *claim* safety (or at least proceed as if safety were established) without proof. **Every one of the five surveyed systems ships this escape hatch as a real, load-bearing part of its ergonomics** — it is not a corner case; SPARK's own documentation walks through `pragma Assume` as a named alternative to the null-procedure and ghost-code options for an un-provable custom lemma [Secondary; access date 2026-07-12], and Dafny/F*'s `assume` is standard, documented, greppable vocabulary. If Precept's design ceiling genuinely forecloses this tier (which its own §221 soundness principle appears to require), **Precept is choosing to forgo the one escape mechanism every surveyed comparator relies on for the residue Tier 2 cannot close inexpensively.** That is a real, identifiable, named cost of the "no opaque solver, no false 'safe' claim" combination — not a hidden one, and not one this file resolves; it is the honest price tag on Principle 1 + Principle 3 held together, surfaced here for the owner-gated design conversation.

### 5. The GATE question: does "unprovable" block the artifact from existing at all?

The five comparators split on a question the brief's framing conflates but the field does not: *does failing to verify stop the program from compiling/running, or is verification a separate, optional certification layer riding alongside a program that exists and runs regardless?*

- **Non-gating** (verification is a decoupled analysis pass; the artifact exists and runs whether or not it verifies): **SPARK/GNATprove** — GNATprove is invoked separately from the Ada compiler; the `Unproved`/`Justified` buckets are *classification of an analysis run*, and the underlying Ada source compiles via GNAT regardless (`proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md:49-59`, already on file). **Frama-C/WP**, **Astrée**, **CBMC**, **Infer** are the same shape — all are static analyzers invoked *on* a C/C++/Java program that already compiles with its ordinary toolchain.
- **Gating** (unverified = the artifact does not exist as compiled/typechecked output): **Dafny** (verification is required by default for `build`/`run`, per `proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md:420-433`, already on file), **F\*** (typechecking *is* verification — an unproven VC is a type error), **Liquid Haskell** (a GHC plugin; a Liquid Type Mismatch is reported as a GHC compiler error, blocking compilation, per `proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md:327-362`, already on file).

**The load-bearing observation:** every comparator in the *gating* cluster (Dafny, F*, Liquid Haskell) is *also* SMT-based. Every comparator that is solver-free or SMT-light for the fragment that matters (SPARK's dedicated Interval bound-propagation pass, EVA, Astrée, InferBO — the abstract-interpretation family already surveyed in `interval-vs-value-evaluation-prior-art-2026-06-05.md`) is *also* non-gating for the properties an SMT-free pass cannot reach — it emits an alarm in a decoupled layer rather than refusing to compile the program. **No system surveyed in either this file or the prior in-tree surveys combines "unprovable blocks the artifact" with "no SMT solver anywhere in the discharge path."** Precept's stated posture — reject at compile time (gating) *and* solver-free (per spec:225's SMT exclusion) — is the conjunction of a property drawn from one cluster and a property drawn from the other. Individually, both properties are mainstream and well-precedented (§ Implications). Their **conjunction**, specifically for the fragment beyond what a bounded, solver-free strategy set can decide, has no direct comparator in this survey.

## Implications for Precept

1. **"We couldn't prove it, so it's forbidden" is not the field's actual answer for nonlinear arithmetic — it never appears as a system's *only* recourse in any of the five comparators.** The actual answer, overwhelmingly, is "supply a decidable fact that discharges the goal" (a lemma, a `calc` chain, an ACSL lemma, a tactic, a reflected proof-as-program), escalating in author cost from "call an existing library lemma" (cheap) to "write and prove a custom lemma" (moderate) to "prove the lemma by hand in an interactive prover" (expensive) — never to a flat refusal with no path at all.

2. **Precept already has the shape of Tier 2 (supply-the-missing-fact) for its existing linear/relational fragment, under its own name: the `rule`.** `precept-language-spec.md:1138` states "`min 5` and `rule X >= 5` are interchangeable to the proof engine" — a `rule` is an author-supplied, `because`-justified fact that becomes a proof premise, exactly the *shape* of a SPARK lemma call or a Dafny `calc` step (an assumed, checked-for-consistency fact the prover then uses downstream), without needing a `lemma`/`ghost`/`tactic` keyword borrowed from the comparator vocabulary. Precept's `type-proof-stage-contract-survey.md:56,120` documents the matching architectural fact: obligations are already catalog-sourced via a `DynamicObligationGenerator`, the same "walk the typed tree, consult catalog-supplied metadata" shape Rust borrowck and Roslyn nullable use — extending that catalog to accept a nonlinear *relational* rule (e.g., a `because`-justified statement that one field is the product of two others) as a discharging premise for a downstream obligation is architecturally continuous with what exists today, not a new construct. Whether the *current* bounded strategy set does this for products/ratios of two non-constant fields is an implementation question (**MERELY-UNIMPLEMENTED**, per the discipline this research operates under), not a *design-ceiling* limit — the comparator precedent (SPARK's lemma-call discharge is itself a bounded, structural pattern-match against a supplied pre/postcondition pair, not a general algebra engine) shows this pattern is achievable **without** opening the SMT door spec:225 forecloses.

3. **Genuinely FUNDAMENTAL limits exist and are narrower than "all nonlinear arithmetic":** integer nonlinear arithmetic (Diophantine solvability) is undecidable in the strict sense (Matiyasevich, resolving Hilbert's Tenth Problem — Tertiary, flagged); no system, Precept included at any design ceiling, can build a total decision procedure for it. Real nonlinear arithmetic is decidable in principle (Tarski–Seidenberg — Tertiary, flagged) but only via quantifier elimination or an SMT nonlinear-real-arithmetic theory — machinery that is **PRINCIPLE-EXCLUDED** for Precept (spec:225's SMT/Z3 exclusion applies to the *mechanism*, independent of whether the underlying problem is decidable), not fundamentally impossible. This distinction matters for the design conversation: "Precept cannot prove `A * B <= C` for two non-constant fields" is true, but it is true for the *principle-excluded* reason (no solver/QE), the *same reason* Precept excludes general linear arithmetic beyond octagon-closure (already established: `interval-vs-value-evaluation-prior-art-2026-06-05.md:65`) — not because the underlying fact is philosophically un-knowable the way an undecidable halting-style question would be.

4. **Precept structurally avoids the single largest escape-mechanism cost class in this entire corpus: loop-invariant/widening annotation burden.** Every system surveyed that handles loops (SPARK's `pragma Loop_Invariant`, Dafny's `invariant`, Frama-C EVA's widening producing false alarms without user-supplied bounds, Astrée's "supply external knowledge (e.g., loop bounds)" — all already documented in `proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md:45-47,152-156,238-240`) pays a real, well-documented ergonomic tax for iteration. Precept's spec:158 ("No loops. The language has no iteration constructs... This eliminates the need for fixpoint computation and widening operators") means the brief's "unbounded accumulation" category **does not currently exist as a computation Precept can express at all** — there is no fold/sum/running-total construct in the language surface (confirmed by grep against `precept-language-spec.md` and `collection-types.md`: no `sum`/`total`/`average` accessor exists; `.count` is the only aggregate, and it is exact by construction, per `collection-types.md:95`). This is not a gap needing an escape mechanism — it is a category Precept has no design-ceiling need to address unless a new aggregate/fold construct is proposed, which would itself be new language surface requiring the CLAUDE.md pre-design owner-consultation gate. This research does not propose such a construct.

5. **Transcendental functions are similarly outside Precept's current design surface** (only `sqrt`/`pow`/`abs` exist as nonlinear-adjacent built-ins, per `docs/compiler/proof-engine.md:522-523,777`, each already carrying a proof-obligation discharge path — `sqrt`'s non-negativity obligation and `pow`'s non-negative-integer-exponent obligation are today's live instances of exactly the "supply the missing fact via a declared modifier/rule/guard" pattern (spec item 4, `precept-language-spec.md`). If transcendental functions (log/exp/sin/cos) were ever proposed, the field's answer (uninterpreted/axiomatized function + a small library of catalog-supplied monotonicity/range lemmas, e.g. SPARK/Dafny's lemma-library pattern) is achievable within a solver-free bounded strategy set — each such lemma is a structural pattern-match, not a general calculus — but this is again new language surface out of scope for this research to design.

6. **The genuinely novel element in Precept's stated posture is not "solver-free" alone, nor "prove-or-reject" alone — both are independently well-precedented (§5's two clusters) — it is their conjunction with the additional exclusion of the Tier-3 trust escape (`assume`/`admit`) every comparator relies on.** SPARK is non-gating and solver-based; Dafny/F*/LH are gating and solver-based; none is gating *and* solver-free *and* without an `assume`-class fallback. If Precept's finished design genuinely forecloses all three of (a) SMT, (b) an unsound `assume`-style trust escape, and (c) gates the artifact on unprovability with no author-fact-supply channel for the nonlinear residue — that specific triple conjunction has no precedent in this survey, and would be a narrower position than any single comparator's, not a bounded instance of the field's normal ergonomics. If, instead, (c) resolves via the `rule`-as-premise channel (Implication 2), the posture is a recombination of well-precedented parts under Precept's own vocabulary, not a novel restriction.

## Conclusions

**Conclusion 1 — The field's actual answer to "auto-proof failed on a nonlinear/undecidable goal" is never blanket forbiddance; it is always a tiered supply-the-missing-fact mechanism, with an (unsound) trust-escape fallback every comparator keeps and Precept's own soundness principle rules out.**

- *Rationale*: five independent, differently-architected systems (SPARK, Dafny, Frama-C/WP, F*, Liquid Haskell) converge on the identical three-tier shape (automatic → supply-a-proved-fact → explicit-unsound-trust), despite having no shared implementation lineage for this specific mechanism.
- *Alternatives considered and rejected*: "some system in this space simply refuses and leaves the author with no path" — searched for directly (SPARK's `Unproved` bucket, Dafny's verification failure, F*'s "unknown," Frama-C's alarm) and in every case a documented escape existed and was exercised in the cited examples; the hypothesis that pure rejection is a real terminal state did not survive the search.
- *Precedent*: Moy 2016 (SPARK lemma library); Dafny FAQ + wiki (calc/opaque/lemmas); Frama-C case study (ACSL lemma + 2 Coq proofs); F* tutorial (fuel/opaque/tactics/assume); Vazou et al. POPL 2018 (refinement reflection).
- *Tradeoff accepted*: this conclusion is about the field's *typical* ergonomics, not a claim that the escape is *cheap* — Tier-2 cost ranges from "call a library lemma" to "write a Coq proof," and that range is itself real evidence for the design conversation's cost question, addressed in Conclusion 2.

**Conclusion 2 — Precept's design ceiling already contains a Tier-2-shaped mechanism (the `rule`) for its existing linear/relational fragment; whether it extends to nonlinear (product/ratio) premises is an open, MERELY-UNIMPLEMENTED design question, not a FUNDAMENTAL or PRINCIPLE-EXCLUDED one.**

- *Rationale*: a `rule` is already interchangeable with a constraint modifier as a proof premise (`precept-language-spec.md:1138`), and obligations are already catalog-sourced via a dynamic-generator channel structurally identical to Rust borrowck's/Roslyn's self-derivation pattern (`type-proof-stage-contract-survey.md:56,120,166`). The comparator precedent shows the discharge step for a supplied nonlinear fact (once available) is typically a bounded structural check, not a general algebra engine — so adding it does not require reopening the SMT question.
- *Alternatives considered and rejected*: treating this as requiring a wholly new `lemma`/`ghost`/`tactic` construct borrowed verbatim from the comparators — rejected as unnecessary; Precept's own `rule` vocabulary already occupies this design slot, and inventing parallel machinery would violate the catalog system's "never maintain parallel keyword lists" discipline (`CLAUDE.md` § Catalog System).
- *Precedent*: SPARK's `Lemma_Mult_Is_Monotonic` (a supplied pre/postcondition pair, checked structurally); the catalog-driven `DynamicObligationGenerator` already shipping in Precept for interval-containment-on-`set` (`type-proof-stage-contract-survey.md:18`).
- *Tradeoff accepted*: this conclusion identifies a *shape* that exists, not a working implementation — extending the strategy set to consume nonlinear rules as premises is real work with its own soundness obligations (does the rule's RHS structurally match the obligation's subject expression, without solving anything), and is explicitly out of scope for this research to design (per the Tier-2/3 language-surface gate).

**Conclusion 3 — Precept's posture is the field's normal bounded tradeoff along two of its three axes (solver-free; prove-or-reject-gates-compilation) and untested along the third (no `assume`-class trust escape); the un-precedented element is specifically the conjunction, not any single axis.**

- *Rationale*: solver-free-and-non-gating (SPARK's Interval pass, EVA, Astrée, InferBO) and solver-based-and-gating (Dafny, F*, Liquid Haskell) are both mainstream, independently well-precedented clusters. No comparator combines solver-free + gating + no-trust-escape. Precept, per its stated principles (spec:225 SMT exclusion; spec:221 no-false-safe-claim), aims at exactly that combination.
- *Alternatives considered and rejected*: "the tradeoff is fully bounded and precedented because SPARK proves it's possible to be sound and solver-free" — rejected as incomplete; SPARK's solver-free layer (the Interval pass) is *not* gating (the Ada program compiles regardless) and does *not* itself attempt nonlinear reasoning (it hands that to Alt-Ergo/CVC5, i.e., to SMT) — so SPARK does not establish the specific conjunction Precept aims at.
- *Precedent*: the gating-cluster/non-gating-cluster split documented in Finding 5, drawn from the raw per-system data already in `proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md`.
- *Tradeoff accepted*: this conclusion is deliberately unresolved — it identifies where Precept's posture sits relative to precedent without recommending which axis (if any) to relax; that recommendation belongs to the owner-gated design conversation this file feeds, not to this research.

## What would change these conclusions

- If a sixth comparator system were found that is simultaneously solver-free, gates compilation on unprovability, and has **no** `assume`-class trust escape (i.e., truly forces the author down the supply-a-fact path with no unsound shortcut), Conclusion 3's "un-precedented conjunction" claim would be wrong, and Precept's posture would have a direct precedent.
- If Precept's existing `rule`/catalog architecture turns out, on closer implementation inspection, to be structurally incapable of consuming a nonlinear (product/ratio) fact as a discharging premise without a full algebra engine (e.g., if the "structural pattern-match, not algebra" claim about SPARK's lemma discharge does not actually transfer to Precept's expression-tree shape), Conclusion 2 would need to move the nonlinear-rule extension from MERELY-UNIMPLEMENTED toward PRINCIPLE-EXCLUDED.
- If the Hilbert's-Tenth-Problem / Tarski–Seidenberg framing in Implications item 3 is wrong in a way that collapses the FUNDAMENTAL/PRINCIPLE-EXCLUDED distinction (e.g., if real nonlinear arithmetic turns out to have no decidable fragment relevant to Precept's actual obligation shapes), the practical recommendation is unaffected (both routes exclude the same discharge mechanism) but the classification language in this file would need correction.

## Open Questions

- Whether Precept's specific fault-floor obligations (divisor safety, sqrt/pow non-negativity) that already use the "supply nonzero/positive/rule/guard" pattern have ever, empirically, hit a case in the `samples/` corpus where the *only* available fact would have to be nonlinear (a product/ratio rule) — this would upgrade Implication 2 from an architectural-shape observation to a demonstrated-need one. Not investigated here; a corpus probe similar to `fragment-boundary-corpus-validation-2026-06-05.md`'s method would answer it.
- Whether an author-facing "explicit, structured, greppable, but unverified" marker (the Tier-3 trust escape) is compatible with Precept's soundness principle if reframed as a *governed* fact rather than a *proved* one — i.e., whether "supply a `rule` that the compiler does not itself verify but the runtime enforces" (which Precept already does for ordinary business rules, per the fault-floor research's governance/proof duality) is actually a *fourth*, Precept-native tier this survey's five comparators don't have a name for, because none of them have a runtime governance layer discharging the same premise their static layer assumed. This is the single most promising open thread for the design conversation and is explicitly not resolved here.
- Whether Frama-C/WP's documented policy (not just the one case-study's practice) states a general position on nonlinear-arithmetic escape mechanisms — the WP reference manual itself was not fetched this session (403 at fetch time); a follow-up with a mirrored PDF or a different access path would strengthen that row from Secondary to Primary.
