# ADVERSARY REPORT — pure prove-or-reject, Q1/Q2, under the RIGHT engine

## Verdict up front

**The pro-reject position survives on both questions under the maximally-legible engine.** I could not construct a FORCES-WORSE counterexample that survives all four remediations available to the no-escape posture (state-as-rule / guard+reject-with-runtime-boolean / boolean-flag-for-record / clamp-where-honesty-permits). Every candidate collapses either to FORCES-BETTER or to a single real ergonomic gap. Q2 has no expressiveness wall *attributable to prove-or-reject itself*; the two genuine walls (row-11 nonlinear×relational, and iterative computation) belong to held-fixed identity commitments (legibility, loop-free execution), not to the posture.

**But the probes over-sell how cleanly they win, in three ways that matter,** and one of them is a genuine spec-anchoring failure of exactly the kind the owner flagged. Ranked below.

---

## F1 — [STRONGEST REAL THREAT] The "state-a-premise / guard+reject" escape that carries every probe's Q1 verdict silently depends on an **unbuilt language construct** (compute-then-check), and the probes fold it in as trivial

Every probe resolves its contingent cases the same way: the author restates the containment condition as a `when`-guard plus a `reject` row (or as a `rule`), the guard evaluates at runtime as a plain boolean needing no proof, and it discharges the subsequent `set` by congruence. This is correct and it is *why* the posture survives — but it forces the author to **duplicate the entire derived formula** (often nonlinear, often long) into the guard, because a row's guard is evaluated before its actions and nothing lets the author name the computed value and route on it. P2 §4.3 and P4 both name this, but P2 folds it into a list of "recommended spec changes" and P4 asserts the posture is "expressively closed" — while its closure demonstrably *depends on a construct that does not exist and is net-new language surface* (Tier-2/3 owner-gated `/design`, not a spec-line edit).

**Why this is the top threat:** it is the one cost that is (a) universal (hits every contingent write in every domain, not a narrow cell), (b) a genuine drift/maintainability hazard for the exact non-developer audience the language targets, and (c) load-bearing — if the construct is *refused*, the posture is materially worse ergonomically everywhere, and the probes' "FORCES-BETTER, just write the guard" verdicts all silently degrade to "FORCES-BETTER but with a duplicated 100-char formula you must keep in sync." The probes treat an unbuilt, un-designed construct as a settled premise. That is the same "assume the fix into existence" pattern the framing warns against, applied to language surface rather than engine power.

**Recommendation:** the pro-reject verdict should be stated as *conditional on* shipping a compute-then-check row form, and that construct routed through the owner gate as the highest-leverage dependency of the whole posture — not listed as one bullet among engine upgrades.

## F2 — [CRITICAL SPEC-ANCHORING] `spec:223` "Proven violations only" is held as **identity** by P2 (and invoked as "false alarms destroy trust") while P2 simultaneously endorses rejecting merely-unprovable writes — a live contradiction reconcilable only via the exact decision under study

`spec:223` states the compiler "reports what is definitively broken, **not what might be broken**," and `spec:229` item 5 defines a distinct third outcome — *unresolved* ("the compiler cannot determine either") — separate from *proved dangerous*. Pure prove-or-reject **collapses `unresolved` into `Error`**: it rejects a write that is not proven broken. In the genuinely-unresolved cell (P4's row 11: a derived value that may be *always safe* yet legibly-unprovable), this is a **false rejection of safe code** — precisely "flagging what might be broken," which `spec:223` calls turning the compiler into a nag.

- **P2** is the sharpest offender: it lists "proven-violations-only (:223)" as *identity-level* with the gloss "false alarms destroy trust," then concludes reject survives — without ever noticing that rejecting the always-safe-but-unprovable write *is* the false alarm its own held-fixed principle forbids. That reconciliation is never performed.
- **The only way to reconcile** reject-on-unproven with `spec:223` is `spec:0.7`'s classification of "no result outside a declared bound" as a **fault-prevention obligation** (line 52: "rejects the definition… there is no deferral"). But `spec:0.7` silently files a *policy-band* overflow (a `max 1000` a value can exceed without the runtime faulting) under the same heading as div-zero — **and that classification is the precise question the whole exercise is challenging.** The framing explicitly lists `:266-272` as not-to-be-held-fixed.
- **Net:** P1's C4 ("§0.7 can stand") and P3's "keep prove-or-reject for proof-carrying bounds unconditionally (spec:266)" are defensible *because they explicitly separate fault-bands from policy-bands first*. P2 does **not** make that separation and inherits `spec:223` as authority while defending a posture that contradicts it. `spec:223` and the fault-classification of policy bands are the two current-spec decisions most in need of *challenge*, and the probes that lean on them to "settle" the coherence of policy-band rejection have anchored on the decision instead of arguing it.

**What the spec should say:** distinguish `unresolved` from `proved-dangerous` in the *diagnostic surface even when both block* (P4's WP-print demand is the right instinct); and decide explicitly whether a non-proof-carrying policy band is a fault (reject) or a governable expectation (the crux) — rather than letting `spec:0.7`'s heading pre-decide it.

## F3 — [Q1 residue] The probes reach **opposite verdicts on the same external-truth structure** and neither states the discriminator; the one acknowledged survivor (P3 Case 5) is real but over-narrowed

P1 Class 4 (external-truth projection, `RemoveJob.Hours`) → **FORCES-BETTER**. P3 Case 5 (calibration-corrected value, truth on a fitted curve) → **FORCES-WORSE (survivor)**. These are the *same* structure: the safety fact lives outside the file. The verdicts diverge because of a discriminator **neither probe names: guard-checkability at ingress.** P1 Class 4's external delta can be range-checked against stored state by a guard the caller can act on → FORCES-BETTER. P3 Case 5's correlation cannot be checked against *anything in the file* → the guard+reject escape is unavailable, so it survives.

But P3 Case 5's cost is **smaller than P3 states** once the full remediation set is applied: for the *record-and-flag* reading it resolves to an unbounded field + derived boolean flag (`IsOutOfSpec <- value > UpperSpec`, no band → no obligation → compiles, records the true value, keeps the flag). It survives as FORCES-WORSE **only** in the intersection: value is *entity-controlled* (not observation, so flag-and-record is a downgrade of a real guarantee) **and** the safety region is *file-inexpressible* (empirically/simulation-characterized, no closed-form rule) **and** the band is a *hard invariant* (clamp is honesty-blocked). In that intersection all escapes fail. **However**, that intersection is bounded by the **one-file identity commitment** (knowledge outside the file is un-provable by *any* engine), so the increment attributable to *prove-or-reject specifically* (vs. govern-with-runtime-check) is only: govern would let the author keep the band and enforce it per-instance at commit, whereas reject must drop it. And even that increment is **erased by guard+reject** (a runtime boolean guard needs no proof and discharges the write by congruence) — leaving, once more, only F1's formula-duplication cost. So P3's survivor is genuine at the *knowledge*-boundary but its *prove-or-reject-specific* cost reduces to F1. The probes should have cross-checked P1 Class 4 against P3 Case 5 and derived the guard-checkability discriminator explicitly; the divergence is an internal inconsistency in the probe set.

## F4 — [Q2 coverage gap, genuine limit] Iterative computations are unwritable, and P2 missed the flagship finance instance while claiming domain coverage

P2 covers amortization *payment* (closed-form, `pow`) but never **yield-to-maturity / internal-rate-of-return**, which has **no closed form** and requires iteration. Under the held-fixed loop-free execution model these are **genuinely unwritable — you cannot compute the value, let alone check a band on it.** Same for Newton root-finding, iterative reserving, any fixpoint. This is a *real* fundamental Q2 limit, but it belongs to the **loop-free execution model** (identity) not to prove-or-reject, so it does not threaten the posture — it does, however, falsify P2's implicit claim to have comprehensively covered finance, and P4's "expressively closed" should explicitly carve out iterative algorithms (it gestures at this via row 12/folds but never names the IRR-class problem, which is the one a finance author will actually hit).

## F5 — [Coverage audit] Domains/shapes the probes did not cover; none breaks the verdicts, but two were resolved only by *this* pass

- **Observation / monitoring / audit** (sensor readings, recorded external metrics whose "band" is an *expectation*): uncovered as a class. Resolves FORCES-BETTER via unbounded-field + derived-boolean-flag (F3). The pro-reject-relevant lesson: prove-or-reject correctly forces the author to distinguish *invariant* (hard band) from *expectation* (value + flag) — a real modeling improvement, including for **stateless precepts** where "route to a state" is unavailable.
- **Resource / interval scheduling with cumulative-capacity or non-overlap invariants** (flagship — samples ship a meeting-scheduler, venue-booking; *no probe analyzed the derived-write-into-structural-collection-invariant shape*). On inspection it does **not** harbor a survivor, and the reason is a *point FOR the posture the probes never made*: Precept writes are deterministic single-valued (`set F = E`), so they pose a *verification* obligation (does this fixed assignment fit?), never an *existence/search* obligation (does some assignment fit?). Verification of a fixed schedule against cumulative capacity is a poly sweep-line check with a legible endpoint-witness; the NP-hard *arrangement* problem never arises. This structurally explains why the cell avoids NP-hardness and should be stated explicitly.
- **Derived structured/format-constrained values** (computed identifiers with check-digits, derived strings into pattern constraints): uncovered, thin, low threat (a computed checksum is decidable by the same expression).
- **Cross-precept / cross-entity derived values**: only touched via P1 Class 4; systematically these are one-file-boundary (S3) cases.
- **Reasoning-quality note:** P1's temporal verdict (Class 7 → NEUTRAL) rests on **corpus-absence** ("zero corpus instances") in a young 77-file corpus, not on analysis. The analysis (date arithmetic is linear → provable given bounds) *supports* NEUTRAL, but P1 reached it from absence, which is the weak-evidence pattern; the conclusion happens to hold.

## Strongest surviving threat, stated once

**The compute-then-check construct gap (F1), compounded by the unexamined `spec:223`/policy-band-as-fault anchoring (F2).** The posture is sound, but its survival is *conditional on a net-new language construct the probes treat as already-granted*, and its *coherence* under the spec's own "proven violations only" principle is *conditional on classifying policy-band overflow as a fault* — the exact decision under challenge. Neither condition is a proof-engine question; both are decisions the owner still has to make. A ratification of pure prove-or-reject that does not (a) commit to the compute-then-check construct and (b) explicitly re-decide `spec:223` + the fault/policy-band classification would be resting on the probes' two weakest, most-anchored moves. Everything else the probes claim — the cell is small, mostly FORCES-BETTER, no clean FORCES-WORSE, no prove-or-reject-specific expressiveness wall — holds under adversarial pressure.
