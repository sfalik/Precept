# Proof-Engine Decision Ledger — for Shane's ruling, 2026-07-12

**What this is.** The decisions that unblock rebuilding the compiler-readiness plan and starting to code the proof engine. This page **surfaces** decisions; it makes none. Where the analyses converged, the lean is stated — as a *recommendation only*. Work each item top to bottom; they're in dependency order. Terms used throughout: **prove-or-reject** = when the compiler can't *prove* a computed value stays inside its declared limit, it rejects the definition outright — nothing is "checked later at runtime." **Certificate** = a short, plain show-your-work note attached to every proof verdict that anyone can re-check by hand without re-running the engine.

## The two keystones (everything else is downstream)

### 1. Ratify prove-or-reject itself

**Question.** When a computed value is written into a field with a declared limit and the compiler can't prove it stays inside: reject the definition (A), or accept it and let the runtime refuse violating writes as they happen (B)?
**Options.** **A — reject** (Frank's ruling, and the follow-up analysis's conclusion). **B — govern**: runtime-enforce limits that no proof depends on; reject only where a proof leans on the limit (the thesis's counter-position, `precept-identity-and-guarantees-thesis-2026-07-11.md:328–334`).
**Unblocks.** Everything below — the MVP, the roadmap, the plan rebuild. If this softens, the whole staging is void (`proof-engine-mvp-and-proof-phases-2026-07-12.md:18`).
**Where argued.** `docs/Working/proof-engine-boundary-ruling-2026-07-06.md` §5 Q5/Q7 (:174–190) — plus its ratification checklist (:307–320).
**Recommendation.** **A.** Frank's ruling, the forcing analysis, and the shipped precedent (count bounds, owner-signed) all land on reject; the thesis converged on the same architecture everywhere *except* this one cell, and the later analysis answered its over-rejection worry by fixing the engine (items 2–3) rather than softening the posture. One condition rides with it: ratifying A over *today's* engine would reject the corpus's own house style — A is honest only as a package with items 2 and 3 (`prove-or-reject-forcing-and-expressiveness-2026-07-12.md:273`).
**Folded into this ruling if you take A** (converged, no separate debate needed): the Hybrid model + "raw input is governed at the door; anything computed from it is proven" boundary; "no deferral" read as fault-scoped (R1 Reading N, ruling :238); the per-proof PROVEN/GOVERNED visibility surface (Q9, :196); the three-category diagnostic taxonomy (old GATE-H); overflow stays parked per old D1 (the one carve-out).
**✅ RULING (Shane, 2026-07-12): A — prove-or-reject, RATIFIED.** Justification: prove-or-reject is part of Precept's unique value proposition and will genuinely push authors to build better precepts, in line with the philosophy. (Ratified as a package with #2 and the MVP engine work, per the condition above.)

### 2. Certificate criterion (spec:225)

**Question.** The spec currently bans solver-style proof engines outright ("SMT/Z3 solvers are excluded even when they could prove more," `precept-language-spec.md:225`). Replace that tool-ban with a *criterion*: any proof is acceptable **iff** it ships a legible certificate — naming the author's own declarations it used and the arithmetic combining them — that an independent tiny checker can replay?
**Options.** **A — adopt the certificate criterion** (replaces the tool-ban sentence). **B — keep the literal ban** (which arguably outlaws even the modest linear reasoning the MVP needs).
**Unblocks.** Legalizes the whole MVP engine: any linear-arithmetic strategy is spec-legal only with such a certificate (`proof-engine-mvp-and-proof-phases-2026-07-12.md:143`).
**Where argued.** `docs/Working/prove-or-reject-forcing-and-expressiveness-2026-07-12.md` §4.1 (:245).
**Recommendation.** **A.** Both analyses and Frank's review converge (`frank-review-proof-engine-mvp-phases-2026-07-12.md:70`); the certificate is *stronger* compliance with the ban's actual intent (legibility) than the tool ban itself. Whether it satisfies the ban is yours to ratify, not theirs to assume.
**✅ RULING (Shane, 2026-07-12): A — adopt the certificate criterion, RATIFIED, with an EXTENSION.** A proof strategy is admissible only if it is all four: (1) **legible & re-checkable** — emits a certificate from a small, spec-enumerated vocabulary, replayable by an independent checker and readable by the author; (2) **performant** — does not break single-file recompile on every debounced keystroke (stay in the ~1–3 ms class against the ~50 ms budget); (3) **right-sized for Precept** — complexity matched to Precept's tiny, few-variable, loop-free problems, not general-purpose machinery; where a procedure could blow up it hits a hard cap and falls back to "couldn't prove — here's what would," never hanging; (4) **justified — earns its place** — each proof strategy added must carry a solid, evidenced justification of real value to Precept, on at least one of: added expressibility for the domains that matter to Precept, a substantial improvement in authoring quality/correctness (better precepts, less scattered logic), or genuine prevention of runtime faults. Nothing is added speculatively or "because we can": technical admissibility (1–3) is necessary but not sufficient — a strategy must also earn its place (4). The engine's power is bounded by what stays legible, fast, right-sized, AND justified at Precept's scale. (This gives the principled basis for §1b's variable-count cap, for excluding heavyweight solver libraries, and for requiring each of §1a and §1b to demonstrate its own benefit before it ships — see #3.)

> **✅ Ratifying 1 + 2 unblocks the MVP core immediately — no other decision blocks it.** The MVP is spec-clean and codeable now: **§1a** (read one richer author-written fact at a time — e.g. a guard on `Total + Amount`), **§2** (case-by-case reasoning: if/else arms, guards on event inputs), **§3** (money through × and ÷ — the one gap with *no* hand-fix today), **§4a** (rejections that show the numbers plus one breaking example), **§5a** (the certificate format, laid down first), and **§6** (let a declared rule do the work — the author states a fact, the engine applies it). Roll-up: `proof-engine-mvp-and-proof-phases-2026-07-12.md:466–477`.
> **✅ MVP SCOPE (Shane, 2026-07-12): §6 pulled into the MVP** (was "first upgrade after MVP"). Rationale: §6 is the philosophy-aligned way to handle multi-fact cases — the author states the consequence as one rule and the engine applies it (no solver, no spec override), which is exactly the honest fix for the declared-bound cases that would otherwise reject with no escape. *Verification owed before the plan: confirm §6 makes bound-containment checks (not only divisor checks) consult declared rules — a bare composite rule does NOT discharge a `min 0` bound today, so the MVP scope must explicitly cover "bound-checks read rules," not only "rules-with-arithmetic are read."*

## The build-scope call

### 3. §1b — combining two or more facts (and the spec:256 override it needs)

**Question.** §1a uses one written-down fact at a time. §1b **combines** facts — e.g. "allocations never exceed commitments" + "commitments stay under plan" together prove a division safe that neither rule proves alone. Build it in the MVP, as the phase right after, or not at all? And building it at all requires you to override a locked spec sentence: reasoning is "single-pass and depth-bounded (no transitive chasing of a third field)" (`precept-language-spec.md:256`) — combining facts is exactly that chasing.
**Options.** **A — in the MVP.** **B — the phase after §1a.** **C — don't build.** (A and B both require authorizing the spec:256 override; the forcing analysis would go further and replace the sentence with full linear reasoning, `prove-or-reject-forcing-and-expressiveness-2026-07-12.md:253`.)
**What's known.** No case in the 77 samples needs it — but the samples were written around the engine's limits, so that silence proves nothing. From first principles, your target domains (blended caps, balance identities, allocations, staged approvals) hit it routinely; for the declared-bound variant there is **no author workaround at all** today. Cost is **size, not speed**: ~3× §1a's build (~1,500–2,200 engine lines), microseconds at compile time. (`proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md:77–95, :126`.)
**Unblocks.** The multi-fact solving core; and settles whether the plan rebuild carries a §1b research phase.
**Where argued.** `docs/Working/proof-engine-linear-solver-feasibility-2026-07-12.md` §2.
**Recommendation.** **B** — a legitimate second phase, not MVP: §1a delivers the shared substrate first, and honest sequencing says don't put the biggest greenfield build in the first slice (feasibility :95; Frank leaned defer, `frank-review-proof-engine-mvp-phases-2026-07-12.md:69`). Noting you've leaned toward *including* it on the domain-patterns argument — B keeps it in, just second. Ruling B (or A) should explicitly authorize the spec:256 override in the same breath.
**✅ RULING (Shane, 2026-07-12): DEFER §1b — leave it OPEN, not in the MVP, not scheduled** (option C). A possibility to consider later, but not a strong enough case to include now — for the reasons from Frank's analysis: combining linear facts is never an *expressibility* gain (the combination of linear inequalities is itself a linear inequality, so the derived bound is always writable as one authored rule); its only benefit is *ergonomic*, and that shrinks once authors name intermediate quantities (which prove-or-reject nudges them to do); the strongest candidates (finance waterfalls / simultaneous-constraint LPs) fall outside Precept's integrity contract; and it is the highest-cost, spec-override-requiring, soundness-critical build in the roadmap. **§6 (now in the MVP) is the honest fix** for multi-fact cases — the author states the consequence, the engine applies it. **`spec:256` override: NOT authorized** (deferred with §1b — build nothing that requires it). **Revisit trigger:** a *recurring* real definition where the derived bound genuinely resists being named as an intermediate field AND authors hand-derive the composite rule wrong often enough to bite in practice.
