### The two proposed conversations

## 2. Audit of the three technical inputs

The inputs are useful lists of things to check, not three independent confirmations. The findings below distinguish a false statement, a valid result used beyond its assumptions, and a gap in the cited evidence. The stated consequence is what would go wrong **if the design adopted the claim**; it is not a report of a compiler run.

### `inputs/technical-proof-model.md`

The central finding at :33-37 stands: a general obligation to establish and preserve every authored rule does not follow from having a list of individual operator preconditions (`proof-engine.md:489,2612-2616`; Want:16-24). The following parts should not be copied into that missing design:

| Input claim | Check and consequence |
|---|---|
| :122: whether a required field without a default means zero “is not stated anywhere I found.” | Spec:2085,2094-2097 prohibits construction without required assignments and calls an early read an “undefined value”. A zero used in constant folding is not proof that the entity starts with zero. Distinguish absent optional, placeholder overwritten on every committing route, and uninitialized required field; otherwise establishment can pass with invented data. |
| :129-133: an untouched rule “carries over with no argument needed”; rows writing nothing “need no obligation.” | At most, no **field-preservation** proof when its dependencies and applicability stay unchanged. A no-field-write arrival can activate a residency ensure (owner: “it has to be one”, Walk:225-235). A rejected row can divide by zero in its message (Walk:733-745). An unmatched event can evaluate several guards. Enroll those evaluation sites even if no version commits. The writer discussion at :77-80,132 also needs the optional third ordering destination (`collection-types.md:1201-1212`), not just a catalog entry for the action. |
| :159,288-296 rates restore and inspection “Full”; “Return a new immutable version every time”. | Spec:2165-2167 makes partial inspection conditional on established premises. `evaluator.md:837-865` simulates an action before discounting an earlier winner. `runtime-api.md:277` trusts data governed when written, not conformance to an unrelated revision. Want:163 itself says “every operation returns a new `Version`”, **as defined in the runtime API**. There, a successful creation carries a version; `InvalidArgs` carries a reason (`result-types.md:123`), and inspection predicts without committing. Preserve both immutability and distinct outcomes; do not manufacture a successful version for a refusal. |
| :183: “periods have no ordering”; :351: “For temporal types there is no premise to prove from”. | `temporal-type-system.md:1329` says “Both operands must be pinned to the same single unit”; the owner's “build both” also covers the collection case. Temporal :628 says no **field-constraint** premise and expressly offers guards and type ranges. Both blanket negatives would unnecessarily shrink proofs or create a needless new-language decision. |
| :314,319-322: “it is not true, and here is a case”; a nonnegative weighted sum gives “the conclusion”; “either is fast.” | Mathematical decidability promises neither a result within the recompile budget nor an executable counterexample from a countermodel to local assumptions. Spec:223-225 requires an honest unresolved verdict at a cap. Linear multipliers over rationals certify suitable non-strict inequalities with coefficient matching and a sufficient constant bound, not all integer consequences. `2x ≤ 1` implies `x ≤ 0` for integer `x`; the rational `x = 1/2` disproves the relaxed implication. Handle integer cuts/congruences, strictness and inconsistent premises explicitly. Bound variables, coefficient bit sizes and case splits; a handful of variables is not a time measurement. |
| :331-332: an unwritten field makes a product “linear for that handler's purposes”; nonzero product is “true for integer and decimal”. | An unchanged rate varies across legal entries; amount times rate is not jointly linear. Under finite decimal rounding to at most 28 places, `10^-16 × 10^-16` rounds to zero. Mathematical integer multiplication preserves nonzero, but a bounded implementation must separately discharge overflow. Keep nonnegativity distinct from strict positivity and use the symmetric write and sign tests in the prior follow-up. Otherwise a certificate can authorize division by a stored zero. |
| :339: “Proofs by identical expression are sound under any deterministic arithmetic.” | Only with the same operands, qualifiers, conversions, arithmetic and relevant time **at both evaluations**. A guard comparing an amount to the current cap tells nothing about that same expression after an exit action changes the cap (Spec:2005-2012). In :340 outward rounding also needs a valid interval enclosure of the operation, including sign cases, zero divisors and range errors. Do not issue a certificate step for text equality alone. |
| :353: “either the representation removes overflow for decimal and integer, or the worked example and its acceptance are wrong”. | The deposit is decimal; it says nothing comparable about an accumulating integer. Plan:166 actually corrects this inference. Separate mathematical numeric meaning from bounded external representation, and demonstrate an ingress/egress boundary if proposing different internal and external ranges. |
| :369: “`each P` survives a removal for free”; “`any P` survives an insertion for free”. | Only when the surviving elements, every other value `P` reads, and its applicability are unchanged. A dequeue also writes its destinations. A predicate comparing remaining entries to a severity limit can become false if that limit is the ordering capture. Set insertion, replacement of an existing lookup value, and insertion into an unchanged collection of plain values are not interchangeable. The table must describe the whole action and its dependencies. |
| :404: an edit “is the one place where a rule is evaluated at all”; :412: “a guard that closes the proof always exists and can be printed.” | Want:22,143 includes arg modifier-rules at ingress, and the later direct-edit ruling still reserves its mechanism. For an unconditional assignment of minus one to a nonnegative field, substitution yields false: no **satisfiable** guard permitting the write repairs it. Distinguish changing the action, supplying a useful premise, and declaring a dead row. A suggested repair must not smuggle in a new rule the author cannot write. |
| :422-446 says the rule table “Needs 2” but “Steps 1, 2 and 3 can proceed in parallel”; :444 calls bank mutations criteria that “can be written down now”. | Collect names in parallel, not type-dependent meanings that rely on undecided arithmetic. Preserve all fourteen existing bank edits as design inputs; two descriptions remove the same repayment-cap condition (Want:121,123). Derive new coverage criteria **after** design sign-off, on the owner's order (Rulings:40-43). Neither an inventory nor fourteen rejections of an already-invalid baseline proves completeness. |

Three further qualifications matter to a builder. “Checking is linear in its size” (:397) is not a complexity argument for discovering the complete set of obligations, arithmetic on large coefficients or validating references. Count those costs separately. “Nothing is evaluated at stateless construction” (:457) conflates *no post-mutation rule sweep* with *no evaluation*: defaults, computed fields and any initial-event inputs still have to produce values (Spec:2085-2090,2147-2151). Finally, “Identical text after substituting the assignments is the minimum” (:330) leaves fault safety of the **guard itself** unspecified. Collecting all argument failures must not evaluate division using an argument already found invalid; short-circuiting must establish a condition before it is consumed. This is owed even if nothing ever commits.

### `inputs/technical-precedent.md`

**Keep the precedent for the induction, not a claim that it decides Precept's language.** The repository copy of *Rodin: An Open Toolset for Modelling and Reasoning in Event-B*, lines 425-429, actually counts establishment and preservation obligations. That supports the decomposition. It does not establish that Precept's checker will enumerate the right obligations, that its arithmetic is exact, or that its search will meet an interactive budget.

| Input claim | Check and consequence |
|---|---|
| :63,226-228,264: “no state is provably unreachable from what was declared”. | That would pass an unknown result. Want:151 requires “Every state is reachable.” The two-counter impossibility argument assumes **unbounded** counters; two fixed-width integer fields and finitely many states alone give a finite, possibly enormous system. Nor is a satisfiable guard enough: create with zero, provide no write, and require one to leave. Keep distinct: no structural route, possible route not yet proved, and demonstrated feasible run. Neither a theorem about unbounded machines nor a fast over-approximation changes which result may be certified. |
| :40,87,147,180: “Exact rationals remove the problem for the prover”. | Only for the prover's own arithmetic. The repository's *Exact Decimal Arithmetic Survey*:29,42-54 gives the range; the recorded small-product case in *Proving a divisor non-zero when the arithmetic can produce zero from non-zero parts*:63-81 fails in the other direction. | :17,83,106: “There is no third outcome.” | The multiplier formulation needs consistent premises or a separate proof of inconsistency (repository copy of *Donut Domains*, lines 517-545). `x ≤ 0` and `x ≥ 1` imply `y ≤ 0` vacuously; no sum of their coefficients introduces `y`, and no violating assignment meets the premises. Count proofs must also distinguish inserting an existing member from growing a set. A rule keyed only by collection action would both accept invalid cases and refuse valid ones. |
| :73-75,91,239: “exact linear reasoning is affordable on every keystroke”. | *Solver-free static-analysis techniques for Precept's proof engine*:25,153 reports roughly 44 ms on an existing corpus and 3 ms for its worst file, not timings of the proposed prover and checker.
