## Numeric representation (exact rational vs decimal, numeric lanes)

- [`language/rational-vs-decimal-exact-numeric-representation.md`](language/rational-vs-decimal-exact-numeric-representation.md) — neutral survey grounding a possible collapse of Precept's three numeric lanes (`integer` / `decimal` / `number`) toward a **single exact type**, with the owner leaning to exact rationals (p/q) and float dropped. Comparator table across 11 native-rational systems (Scheme/RnRS, Racket, Common Lisp, Clojure, Haskell `Data.Ratio`, Python `fractions`, Ruby `Rational`, Julia `//`, Raku `Rat`, Smalltalk, Wolfram): **only Raku makes a rational the default for a decimal-looking literal** — everywhere else rational is opt-in or arises only from exact integer division. **Key results:** (1) Precept's `decimal`/`number`/`approximate()` structure already **is** the Scheme exact/inexact tower with a named bridge — the proposal only moves the *exact level's representation* and asks to delete the *inexact level*; (2) the **transcendental gap blocks "drop float"** — `sqrt`/`log`/`trig` yield irrationals with no exact p/q home, so an inexact escape hatch survives unless Precept refuses transcendentals or becomes a symbolic CAS (Precept already quarantines `sqrt` in the number lane); (3) **rationals win for the prover** — closed under ÷ (decimal is not: BigDecimal throws / System.Decimal silently rounds `1/3`), matching SMT-LIB Real semantics (values = coprime p/q), so a *rational internal proof-engine representation* is a separable clean win vs the current `.NET decimal` `NumericRuleFact`; (4) **heavy counter-evidence** — finance/regulation converged on **decimal** (Cowlishaw/IBM: Euro regs "dictate the working precision (in decimal digits) and rounding method"; 98.6% of commercial-DB numbers are decimal/integer), no business-rule engine stores rationals, and rational's exact-division *defers* the decimal-rounding decision regulation requires be *made* — arguably anti-honesty; (5) ~~**audience mismatch** — fraction syntax/display is programmer-culture~~ — **this leg was WITHDRAWN 2026-07-20 as uncited and as conflating storage evidence with notation; see `language/business-fraction-vs-decimal-notation.md` below, which tested it against primary sources and found neither it nor its inverse supported.** (The separable technical point stands: a Raku-style decimal-syntax/rational-backing/decimal-display hybrid re-imports a float fallback + denominator ceiling.) Presents four tradeoff clusters (rational author-facing type / decimal-only-drop-float / Raku hybrid / internal-prover-only), no decision. **Precept-internal legs corrected 2026-07-20:** the survey missed that Precept **already ships an exact rational** — `UcumExactFactor` (GCD-reduced `BigInteger` p/q + base-10 exponent) carries every UCUM unit scale and composes exactly through prefix/derived-unit reduction, collapsing to `decimal` only at application; `UcumAtom.ScaleIsRational` already tracks irrational scales transitively (so Area 4's transcendental gap is partly handled at the catalog level); `tools/Precept.Bench` already exists to measure decimal-vs-arbitrary-precision; and exact-rational scales over float factors is **prior locked design**. This makes the question one of *how far to carry* the existing rational, not whether to introduce one. **Cited** — feeds a forthcoming `/design` on numeric-lane simplification; builds forward from the exact-decimal-arithmetic, interval-vs-value, and solver-free-static-analysis surveys. Language-spec / stdlib / SMT-LIB / Cowlishaw / Precept-source legs Primary; Wolfram Secondary; bignum-performance leg Tertiary (see Threats). Load-bearing excerpts mirrored to `references/rational-numeric-representation/`.

- [`language/business-fraction-vs-decimal-notation.md`](language/business-fraction-vs-decimal-notation.md) — evidence survey on how exact non-integer quantities are **notated** in business, legal, financial and regulatory practice, rigorously separating **notation** (what a human writes) from **display** (what a system renders) from **storage** (settled as decimal-dominant by the doc above, cited not re-derived). Commissioned to test that doc's uncited "fraction notation is programmer culture, decimal is universal business notation" claim, which was withdrawn. **Verdict: neither that claim nor its inverse survives; the split is by kind of quantity** — fractions for definitionally-exact shares of a whole, decimals for measured, priced, or computed magnitudes. **Fraction side (all Primary):** deed and mineral conveyances use "words (numeral)" form ("one sixteenth (1/16)") stably from 1891 to 1977 and still litigated verbatim in 2026; Uniform Probate Code uses word fractions; Del. Code tit. 8 § 203 writes "66 2/3%"; FOMC writes "1/4 to 1/2 percent"; **16 CFR § 500.17 affirmatively authorizes common fractions and names the permitted denominators**; Treasuries still quote in 32nds; NIST HB44 documents binary-fraction subdivision in the building trades; **two mainstream ERPs independently store unit-of-measure conversion as an exact integer ratio pair** — SAP `MARM` `UMREZ`/`UMREN` (whole numbers only, user types two integer fields) and Dynamics AX2012/Commerce Runtime `Int32 Numerator`/`Denominator`, both keeping a decimal factor alongside rather than instead of the pair. **Decimal side:** currency, prices, posted ledger amounts, equity percentages, bond conversion ratios; **OMG DMN/FEEL — the standard for business-analyst decision logic — has no fraction literal** (separate `numeric literal` and `division` grammar productions) over IEEE decimal128; SEC decimalization of equities/options (2001) with Reg NMS Rule 612 locking out sub-penny quoting; no surveyed tool stores an exact rational. **Key boundaries:** the trades fraction convention is explained by NIST HB44 as a function of the *measuring instrument and downstream arithmetic*, not audience — machining and surveying go decimal within the same unit system, and metric construction eliminates fractions; ERP ratio pairs appear only in conversion/scaling contexts, **never in posted financial amounts** (SAP `BSEG-DMBTR`, Oracle `GL_JE_LINES`, D365 `Real`, Workday `Get_Journals` are all fixed-scale decimal), and even within conversion contexts most systems evaluate the ratio and round (Oracle/Workday FTE, SAP payroll factoring) — the two exact pairs are the exception. SAP `TCURR` `FFACT`/`TFACT` were specifically **refuted** as a rational: they are integer unit-scaling factors on a decimal rate. For non-terminating values the regulatory pattern (Council Reg. (EC) 1103/97) is fix a finite working precision → defer rounding → round the payable amount → disclose; **no authority calls a truncated decimal an error**. **Comprehension literature does not transfer** (children, probabilistic risk, or small lab samples) and should not be cited in either direction. Proposes no decision. **Active — horizon groundwork**; feeds the same numeric-lane `/design` as the doc above. Load-bearing excerpts mirrored to `references/business-fraction-notation/`.

## Proof engine, proof discharge

- [`architecture/compiler/proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md`](architecture/compiler/proof-engine-interval-arithmetic-survey.md) — interval arithmetic strategies.
- [`architecture/compiler/divisor-nonzero-under-finite-representation-survey.md`](architecture/compiler/divisor-nonzero-under-finite-representation-survey.md) — proving a divisor non-zero when the arithmetic can produce zero from non-zero parts; what SPARK and the SMT-LIB float/real split do about it, and which representations remove the case.
- [`architecture/compiler/underflow-in-solver-free-interval-domains.md`](architecture/compiler/underflow-in-solver-free-interval-domains.md) — narrow follow-up to the divisor survey above, filling the hole that survey named in its own Threats to Validity: **how do the sound, solver-free abstract-interpretation analyzers (Astrée, Frama-C EVA) model arithmetic that rounds to exactly zero, and can any of them prove a computed divisor non-zero?** **Key results:** (1) **the interval domain does not reason over exact reals** — Miné (ESOP 2004) defines the smallest representable magnitude `mf_f` per format and adds it as an absolute error term `mf_f[−1;1]` to *every* operation in the linearization ("an absolute error mf_f due to a possible underflow"), while the plain non-relational interval domain gets there more directly by computing bounds with outward rounding in the concrete format, so a product of two tiny intervals is an interval containing zero *because the stored product genuinely is zero*; Astrée's own paper names treating floats as reals or as fixed-point (CBMC) as "unsound"; (2) **underflow is never itself reported** — Miné's model "tolerates" roundings and underflows while treating division by zero as an error, and EVA's float alarms cover only NaN and infinity (`-warn-special-float` has no underflow setting), so the zero flows silently into the value and the report arrives at the *division*; (3) **the divisor check is a membership test on the interval and refuses when zero is in it** — Miné's interval division returns the run-time error when `b⁻ ≤ 0 ≤ b⁺`, and EVA's own worked example is structurally Precept's case (`A = 100 / (x * y)` → `assert (int)(x*y) ≢ 0`, an obligation about the *whole* divisor expression); the reporting model differs from Precept's, though — EVA emits the alarm and then **continues assuming it does not happen**, where Precept refuses; (4) **a magnitude-lower-bound domain exists and is named** — the **absolute-value octagon domain (AVO)**, Chen/Liu/Miné/Kapur/Wang SAS 2014, infers `±X±Y ≤ c, ±X±|Y| ≤ d, ±|X|±|Y| ≤ e` at the octagon's complexity class with no solver, motivated in its own § 1 by exactly this problem ("to remove a division-by-zero false alarm, the analysis needs to find a range excluding 0 for the divisor, which is in general a non-convex property"), with a results table showing it infer `|den| > 0.1` / `|d| ≥ 0.1` and clear the divisor; the **donut domain** (VMCAI 2012, convex set minus a convex hole) does the same job differently, and there is a dedicated **NECLA Division-by-zero False Alarms** benchmark set; **(5) but neither derives the bound from arithmetic** — every benchmark case originates in a guard the programmer wrote, AVO's constraint language has no rule relating `|X × Y|` to `|X|` and `|Y|`, and *even a correct real-arithmetic bound would not finish the job*: the stored value is the rounded product, so concluding it is non-zero requires the derived bound to clear the format's smallest magnitude — a comparison against a representation constant, not an algebraic step, performed by no surveyed system; (6) **exactness of the lane changes the mechanism, not the existence of the case** — ACSL's logic `real` has "no overflows and no rounding errors" and genuinely removes it, but IEEE 754 *decimal* carries subnormals, a fixed-scale decimal still has a smallest magnitude, and EVA's own divisor example is `int` (wraparound and truncating division are the integer routes to the same zero); the real axis is whether the representation has a smallest positive magnitude or can wrap, which only arbitrary-precision rationals and integers avoid. **Flags but does not design:** Precept has no way to state `|x| ≥ k` today (`nonzero` is a disequality, `min`/`max` bound value not magnitude) — that would be new language surface and belongs in an owner conversation. **Cited.** Miné ESOP'04 / Astrée ESOP'05 / AVO SAS'14 / donut VMCAI'12 / Frama-C EVA manual legs Primary, all excerpts re-extracted with `pdftotext` and mirrored to `research/references/solver-free-static-analysis/`; the IEEE 754 standard itself was paywalled and is Tertiary as used (see Threats).
- [`architecture/compiler/overflow-in-solver-free-interval-domains.md`](architecture/compiler/overflow-in-solver-free-interval-domains.md) — the overflow counterpart to the underflow survey above, same comparators and same interval endpoints, asking the mirror question: **how does a sound solver-free abstract-interpretation analyzer prove the absence of arithmetic overflow, and what does the choice of numeric representation do to that problem?** **Key results:** (1) **comparing the interval against the type's limits really is the whole technique, and it is not even a separate step** — Miné's rounding function is defined to return the run-time error `Ω` exactly when the real result exceeds `Mf_f`, and the interval operations lift that to their endpoints (`[a⁻;a⁺] ⊕♯ [b⁻;b⁺] = Ω if a⁻⊕b⁻ = Ω or a⁺⊕b⁺ = Ω`), so overflow-absence falls out of the abstract arithmetic; Astrée's own design history says the plain interval abstraction "is precise enough to express the absence of runtime errors" and every later domain was about reaching a tighter interval, not a different property; note the sharp contrast with the sibling — Miné's model "tolerates" underflow in the same sentence that lists overflow as a run-time error; (2) **an overflow obligation is unusually forgiving of imprecision** — the same rate-limiter program is discharged by three different bounds, `[−128,128]` (true), `[−136,136]` (octagons, "which is sufficient to prove that Y does not overflow") and `[−261,261]` (Astrée, widening with thresholds); any bound inside the limit is a complete proof, and the named remedies for a bound that is too wide are relational domains, widening with thresholds, variable packing ("average pack size … 3 or 4 variables") and trace partitioning, getting Astrée to zero false alarms on 400,000 lines; (3) **trapping is strictly easier to analyse than wrapping, and the reason is the post-state, not the check** — a trap halts, so the successor state only describes the mathematically correct value the domain already computes, whereas a wrap always succeeds with a value whose image under an interval abstraction is not an interval; EVA alarms on signed overflow (undefined in C99) and *not* on unsigned (defined), and documents the precision cost of the defined case in its gauges section ("the analysis works better when arithmetic overflows are reported as an alarm … the affine relations … are no longer true once the variable exceeds e.g. INT_MAX and wraps"); **and whether overflow is an error at all is a specification choice, not a hardware fact** — Astrée rejects it "although, on the specific platform, the result can be well-defined through modular arithmetics"; saturating arithmetic is treated by nothing surveyed; (4) **an unprovable overflow is an alarm that is simultaneously an assumption** — EVA "emits alarms for — and reduces the sets of possible results of — signed arithmetic computations", visibly so (`f ∈ [2e9..3e9]` but `__retres ∈ [2000000000..2147483647]`), and states "each alarm, in general, is also an assumption"; the reduction is load-bearing for downstream precision, which is why report-and-continue is not a dial-setting away from Precept's refusal but a different internally-consistent model; (5) **arbitrary precision does not remove overflow — it relocates the boundary and converts the failure into a resource question nobody in this family proves** — Java `BigInteger` is "arbitrary-precision" in its first sentence and documents a maximum with `ArithmeticException` in its third paragraph; GMP has "no practical limit … except the ones implied by the available memory" and on allocation failure "terminates the program" with no defined recovery; CPython's values are unbounded but base-10 conversion is sub-quadratic and was **CVE-2020-10735**, fixed by a configurable 4300-digit cap; and the PPL — an analyzer's own numeric library — **defaults to unbounded coefficients** with native integers as an opt-in speed choice guarded by "systematic (yet efficient) overflow detection", paying for the default with runtime watchdog timers and memory caps rather than a proof; (6) **the temporal case is not analytically special** — `java.time.Instant.plus` throws two different exceptions (`ArithmeticException` for the backing `long`, `DateTimeException` for the declared range) while Noda Time calls it overflow outright (`throw new OverflowException("Operation would overflow range of Instant")`), and in both the check is the same endpoint-versus-declared-constant comparison as a type limit. **Flags but does not design:** bounding the resource cost of an unbounded computation would rest on a magnitude bound, and temporal point types carry no bound vocabulary — both are language surface and belong in an owner conversation. Takes **no position** on Precept's open fixed-width-versus-arbitrary-precision question. **Cited.** Miné ESOP'04 / Astrée ESOP'05 / Frama-C EVA manual / PPL SCP'08 / Java SE 21 `BigInteger` + `Instant` / CPython 3.14 / GMP 6.3.0 / Rust Reference + std / Noda Time source / IKOS legs Primary and mirrored to `research/references/solver-free-static-analysis/` and `research/references/arbitrary-precision-numerics/`; the AbsInt product page is Secondary and IEEE 754 was paywalled and is Tertiary as used (see Threats).

- [`architecture/compiler/bounds-only-constraint-enforcement-2026-06-05.md`](architecture/compiler/bounds-only-constraint-enforcement-2026-06-05.md) — evaluates the **"bounds-only"** hypothesis: should the compiler reason only over field intervals (narrow + default-vs-narrowed-interval + reject-unbounded-source) and **never** fold specific values, retiring the default value-fold (BUG-027)? - [`architecture/compiler/static-vs-runtime-expression-evaluation-survey.md`](architecture/compiler/static-vs-runtime-expression-evaluation-survey.md) — when a system evaluates the *same* expressions at compile/analysis time AND at runtime, how does it keep the two from disagreeing? Neutral spectrum survey across five points: **identical engine** (Rust one MIR interpreter for CTFE + Miri; Zig comptime = ordinary Zig at semantic-analysis time), **shared-definition restricted subset** (C++ `constexpr` — standard *mandates* "same result as an equivalent non-constexpr function" but explicitly carves out floating point as "unspecified"), **abstract soundly-related** (the interval/octagon domain — sound-by-approximation, never claims the runtime value; cited from the interval-vs-value sibling), **independently-implemented conformance-tested** (CompCert semantic-preservation theorem against one mechanized semantics; WASM reference interpreter + test suite), and the **drift pole** — the load-bearing empirical warnings: GCC/LLVM cross-compiler "constant folding must emulate the target machine's arithmetic" (host FP ≠ target FP) and Liquid Haskell's default unbounded-SMT-`Int` vs fixed-width machine-`Int` overflow gap. **Q-B (enforce-statically-vs-defer):** CUE/Dhall/C++ concretely evaluate non-abstractable constraints (equality/string/multi-field) on *constants* statically, but **only alongside a shared or mandated-equal runtime semantics**; Nickel is the runtime-deferral pole. Key synthesis: *concrete static evaluation and a shared/mandated-equal runtime semantics travel together — no surveyed system concretely evaluates statically while leaving an unrelated runtime evaluator free to disagree (that is the drift pole)*. Grounds the Precept tension that `EvaluateBinaryOp` (proof fold) and the catalog `BinaryExecutors` (runtime) are two arithmetic implementations of the same operations. **Active — horizon groundwork**; feeds a `/design` decision on whether the proof-engine fold and runtime evaluator share one expression-evaluation core. Cross-links [`runtime-evaluator-architecture-survey.md`](#compilation-intermediate-representation-runtime-model) and the bounds-only / interval-vs-value siblings. Rust/Zig/C++-draft/GCC/LiquidHaskell/CompCert/WASM legs Primary; Dhall surfaced-not-deep-fetched; Zig "same-semantics" framing partly Tertiary (see Threats to Validity).
